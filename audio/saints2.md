Additions only below. I am not repeating the exact titles from the earlier saint list, except where a related course/book gives access to different saints or broader saint-cult history. Audible has a lot of saint material, but much of it is devotional, children’s material, miracle collections, or Virtual Voice. I separated those from the stronger historical or lecture-style options.

Priority key:
P1 = strongest historical / scholarly / critical fit
P2 = serious but more sympathetic, primary-source-heavy, or lecture/contextual
P3 = useful supplement, popular-level, or primary text
P4 = defer: devotional, short, children’s, Virtual Voice, or weak historical-critical fit

1. Strongest New Catholic / Western Saint Additions

* P1 — St. Ambrose of Milan — Patrick Boucheron, Trace and Aura: The Recurring Lives of St. Ambrose of Milan. This looks like one of the better new finds: more reception history and critical historical reflection than simple hagiography.  ￼
* P1 — St. Clare of Assisi — Joan Mueller, Clare of Assisi: Her Life and Theology. Strong lecture-style option; Audible describes Mueller as a scholar/translator of Clare who explores Clare’s writings, life, times, and spirituality.  ￼
* P1 — St. Dominic — Richard Woods, St. Dominic and the Dominican Way: The Order of Preachers. Good because it covers Dominic, the Dominican order, Albert the Great, Aquinas, Eckhart, Lagrange, Congar, Schillebeeckx, and Dominican controversies rather than treating Dominic in isolation.  ￼
* P1 — William of Norwich / contested saint-cult history — E. M. Rose, The Murder of William of Norwich. Not a clean “official saint biography,” but very important historically: a critical study of a medieval murder accusation, saint-cult formation, ecclesiastical politics, and anti-Jewish blood libel.  ￼
* P2 — St. Louis IX of France — Jean de Joinville and Geoffrey de Villehardouin, Chronicles of the Crusades. Primary-source rather than modern biography, but Joinville’s Life of Saint Louis is a major source for Louis IX, crusading kingship, sanctity, and medieval royal piety.  ￼
* P2 — St. Bonaventure — William J. Short, Saint Bonaventure: The Soul’s Journey into God. More theological lecture than biography, but useful for Bonaventure as Franciscan theologian, mystic, and Doctor of the Church.  ￼
* P2 — St. Anselm of Canterbury — Benedicta Ward, Anselm of Canterbury. Scholar-written and relevant, but the Audible version uses Virtual Voice, so I would treat it as a lower-audio-quality supplement.  ￼
* P2 — St. John of the Cross — John of the Cross: Poet and Mystic. Lecture-style introduction to his life, works, and place in the Christian mystical tradition.  ￼
* P2 — St. Francis de Sales / Salesian spirituality — St. Francis de Sales and the Salesians: A Spirituality for the Modern World. Useful for Francis de Sales, Margaret Mary Alacoque, John Bosco, and the Salesian tradition, though more spirituality-history than critical biography.  ￼
* P2 — St. Frances Xavier Cabrini — Theodore Maynard, The World Is Too Small. Substantial older biography of Mother Cabrini; not as critical as the best modern academic works, but stronger than most saint-devotional audio.  ￼
* P2 — St. Charles de Foucauld — Little Sister Kathleen of Jesus, The Universal Brother. Sympathetic but worthwhile; uses Foucauld’s correspondence and diaries, and gives a serious account of his unusual path from soldier/explorer to Saharan ascetic.  ￼
* P2 — St. Padre Pio — C. Bernard Ruffin, Padre Pio: The True Story. Probably the most substantial Audible biography of Padre Pio; still miracle-friendly and Catholic-sympathetic, so not fully critical in the academic sense.  ￼
* P2 — St. Faustina Kowalska — Sister Sophia Michalenko, The Life of Faustina Kowalska. Authorized biography with diary material; historically useful for Divine Mercy, but more devotional than critical.  ￼
* P2 — St. Pius V — Roberto de Mattei, Saint Pius V. Substantive for Counter-Reformation Catholicism, Lepanto, Tridentine reform, and papal sanctity; likely more traditionalist/confessional than neutral academic history.  ￼

2. Newer / Modern Catholic Saints and Near-Saints

* P2 — St. Carlo Acutis — Nicola Gori, Carlo Acutis. Better than many short popular titles on Acutis, but still closer to contemporary Catholic biography than critical history. Carlo Acutis and Pier Giorgio Frassati were canonized together on September 7, 2025.  ￼
* P3 — St. Pier Giorgio Frassati — Bob and Penny Lord, Blessed Pier Giorgio Frassati. The Audible title still uses “Blessed,” but Frassati was canonized on September 7, 2025; treat this as devotional background, not a critical biography.  ￼
* P3 — St. Josemaría Escrivá / Opus Dei context — Escrivá de Balaguer. Audible has a Spanish item noting Escrivá as Opus Dei’s founder and a figure “revered by some and criticized by others,” but I did not find a strong English critical Audible biography.  ￼
* P3 — St. Josemaría Escrivá / institutional context — Charles River Editors, Opus Dei and the Jesuits. More useful for Opus Dei as an institution than for Escrivá as a saint biography.  ￼
* P4 — St. Junípero Serra — Bob and Penny Lord, Saint Junipero Serra. Exists on Audible, but it is not the critical historical biography you would ideally want for Serra, mission history, Indigenous history, and canonization controversy.  ￼
* P4 — St. Junípero Serra — Christian Clifford, Saint Junipero Serra / Who Was Saint Junipero Serra? Audible has Virtual Voice items, but I would defer them unless you want quick devotional summaries.  ￼

3. Additional Catholic Saints — Useful but Lower Historical-Critical Fit

* P3 — St. Anthony of Padua — Percy Bennington, History in 30: The Life of St. Anthony of Padua. Short, popular, and useful only as a quick orientation.  ￼
* P3 — St. Anthony of Padua — Charles Warren Stoddard, The Wonder-Worker of Padua. Older hagiographical treatment; useful for reception/devotion more than history.  ￼
* P3 — St. Brigid of Kildare — Charles River Editors, Saint Brigid of Kildare. Useful as a short popular overview of Brigid’s legend, Irish Christianity, and the overlap of Christian and pre-Christian Irish tradition.  ￼
* P3 — St. Brigid / early Irish Church — Justin R. McCarthy, Saint Brigid, the Celts and the Early Irish Church. More directly contextual, but it uses Virtual Voice, so I would not rank it highly.  ￼
* P3 — St. Nicholas of Myra — Charles River Editors, Saint Nicholas of Myra: The Life and Legacy of the Ancient Christian Bishop Who Became the Inspiration for Santa Claus. Useful for the Nicholas-to-Santa reception-history angle, but popular-level.  ￼
* P3 — St. John Bosco — Forty Dreams of St. John Bosco. Primary/devotional material from Bosco rather than biography; useful if you want to understand Bosco’s spiritual imagination and later Salesian devotion.  ￼
* P3 — St. Dominic Savio — St. John Bosco, The Life of Dominic Savio. Primary hagiographic biography by Bosco; historically relevant, but not critical.  ￼
* P3 — St. Claude La Colombière — Claude La Colombière, Seeking the Heart of Christ. Primary spiritual reflections from the Jesuit saint associated with St. Margaret Mary Alacoque and Sacred Heart devotion.  ￼
* P4 — St. Martin de Porres — Bob and Penny Lord, Saint Martin de Porres. Devotional biography; useful if you want the figure represented on Audible, but not historical-critical.  ￼
* P4 — St. Rose of Lima — Mary Fabyan Windeatt, St. Rose of Lima. Children’s / devotional treatment; defer for your stated purpose.  ￼
* P4 — St. Margaret Mary Alacoque — Bob and Penny Lord, Saint Margaret Mary Alacoque. Devotional Sacred Heart-focused treatment, not critical biography.  ￼
* P4 — St. Bernadette Soubirous — Lourdes: The Miraculous Life of St. Bernadette Soubirous. Virtual Voice and miracle-forward; defer unless you specifically want Lourdes devotional material.  ￼
* P4 — St. Bridget of Sweden — Francesca Maria Steele, St. Bridget of Sweden. Older devotional / hagiographic style; not a priority for critical history.  ￼
* P4 — St. Philip Neri — Bob and Penny Lord, Saint Philip Neri. Exists on Audible, but it is a devotional saint-life rather than scholarly biography.  ￼

4. Franciscan / Dominican / Medieval Order Tracks

These are good because they cover clusters of saints rather than one biography at a time.

* P1 — Franciscan saints and theologians — William J. Short, The Franciscan Intellectual Tradition. Covers figures such as St. Anthony of Padua, St. Bonaventure, Bl. John Duns Scotus, Roger Bacon, and Bl. Ramon Llull; very useful for medieval Franciscan intellectual holiness.  ￼
* P2 — Francis / Clare / early Franciscan movement — William J. Short, St. Francis of Assisi: A New Way of Being Christian. Francis himself was already on the earlier list, but this course adds Franciscan-movement context and Clare-related material.  ￼
* P2 — Dominican saints and theologians — Richard Woods, St. Dominic and the Dominican Way. Worth listing again here as an order-level track: Dominic, Albert the Great, Aquinas, Eckhart, Lagrange, Congar, Schillebeeckx, and Dominican history.  ￼
* P2 — Medieval Christian intellectual saints — Great Minds of the Medieval World. Includes major medieval Christian thinkers such as Augustine, Ambrose, Aquinas, and others; useful as intellectual context rather than saint biography.  ￼
* P2 — Monastic saints / ascetic culture — Stephen J. Davis, Monasticism: A Very Short Introduction. Useful for understanding Antony, Pachomius, desert monasticism, and later saint-making around monastic holiness.  ￼

5. Eastern Orthodox / Pre-Schism Saints — Additional Audible Material

For Eastern saints, the strongest Audible material is usually primary texts plus context, not modern critical biography.

* P2 — St. Maximus the Confessor — St. Maximus the Confessor. Audible describes this as including Maximus’s theological works plus interpretation by Polycarp Sherwood, a 20th-century Benedictine scholar. This is better than a simple devotional summary.  ￼
* P2 — St. Gregory Palamas — The Triads. Primary theological source rather than biography; important for Hesychasm, Eastern Orthodox theology, and Palamite controversy.  ￼
* P2 — St. John of Damascus — An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith. Primary source; useful for late patristic theology and the synthesis of earlier Greek fathers.  ￼
* P2 — St. Cyril of Jerusalem — The Catechetical Lectures of St. Cyril of Jerusalem. Primary source for fourth-century catechesis, liturgy, and baptismal instruction.  ￼
* P2 — St. Athanasius of Alexandria — On the Incarnation. Primary text; historically important for Athanasius and Nicene theology, though not biography.  ￼
* P2 — St. Athanasius of Alexandria — The Monastic Letters of St. Athanasius the Great. Primary material with monastic context; useful for Athanasius’s relation to Egyptian asceticism.  ￼
* P2 — St. John Chrysostom — Sermons from the Life of St. John Chrysostom. Useful because the sermons are selected to illuminate moments in Chrysostom’s life and character, but still primarily source material.  ￼
* P2 — St. John Chrysostom — Treatise on the Priesthood. Primary text; useful for Chrysostom’s ecclesiology and pastoral theology.  ￼
* P2 — St. Gregory of Nyssa — On the Making of Man. Primary source, not biography; useful for Cappadocian theology and anthropology.  ￼
* P3 — St. Basil the Great / St. Gregory Nazianzen — Basil and Gregory. Appears to be a popular two-in-one life/teaching overview; use cautiously unless you want basic orientation.  ￼
* P3 — St. Macrina the Younger — Macrina the Younger for Beginners. Interesting because Macrina is crucial for the Cappadocian family, but this Audible item uses Virtual Voice.  ￼
* P4 — St. Basil the Great — Basil the Great for Beginners. Virtual Voice; defer unless you want a quick low-cost introduction.  ￼
* P4 — St. Maximus the Confessor — Maximus the Confessor for Beginners. Virtual Voice; use the Maximus/Sherwood item above first.  ￼
* P4 — St. Gregory Palamas sermons — multiple Audible entries exist, but several are Virtual Voice; prioritize The Triads if choosing one Palamas item.  ￼

6. Desert Fathers / Desert Mothers / Egyptian and Coptic Monastic Saints

This is where you get closer to Oriental Orthodox and Coptic holy-person history, though often through primary traditions rather than critical biography.

* P2 — St. Antony, St. Pachomius, early Egyptian monasticism — Nicholas Buxton, The Wisdom of the Desert. Lecture-style material on the origins of Christian monasticism, Antony, Pachomius, and institutional monasticism.  ￼
* P2 — Desert Fathers and Mothers — The Sayings of the Holy Desert Fathers. Primary sayings collection; useful for Antony, Pachomius, Macarius, Moses the Black, and desert ascetic traditions.  ￼
* P3 — Desert saints generally — Fr. Philip Bochanski, Heroes of the Desert: Lives, Faith, and Teachings of the Church Fathers and Mothers. Confessional lecture set; useful as a guided orientation to Antony, Mary of Egypt, and the desert world.  ￼
* P3 — St. Macarius the Egyptian — Fifty Spiritual Homilies. Primary spiritual text; not biography, but important for Egyptian monastic spirituality and later Orthodox mystical tradition.  ￼
* P3 — St. Mary of Egypt — Living for Christ, Vol. II: Saint Mary of Egypt. Audible has a Mary of Egypt item, but it is not historical-critical; use as devotional / hagiographic supplement only.  ￼
* P3 — Coptic Orthodox theological figures — The Coptic Orthodox Church as a Church of Erudition & Theology. Contextual rather than biographical, but useful for Alexandrian and Coptic figures such as Clement, Origen, Athanasius, Cyril, Dioscorus, Macarius, and Didymus.  ￼
* P3 — St. Cyril VI / modern Coptic holiness — Fr. Tadros Y. Malaty, Encounters of Love and Faith with Saint Cyril VI. Coptic Orthodox devotional / contemporary holy-person material; Virtual Voice, so not a high-priority critical source.  ￼
* P3 — Matthew the Poor / Fr. Matta El-Meskeen — Words for Our Lives. Not officially a saint, but important as a modern Coptic monastic elder and influential spiritual writer; use as “holy person / modern monastic” context.  ￼

7. Oriental Orthodox / Syriac Watchlist Notes

I did not find strong English Audible critical biographies for several major Oriental Orthodox or Syriac figures. For these, the best route is usually print, academic articles, or primary texts rather than Audible.

* Watchlist — St. Ephrem the Syrian — major Syriac saint, hymnographer, theologian, and Doctor of the Church, but I did not find a strong Audible critical biography in this pass.  ￼
* Watchlist — St. Shenoute of Atripe — major Coptic monastic figure; I did not find a strong Audible biography. Use print scholarship if you want Shenoute seriously.
* Watchlist — St. Cyril of Alexandria — Audible has Cyril of Alexandria, but it appears to be a basic/possibly popular treatment rather than a major critical biography.  ￼
* Watchlist — St. Dioscorus of Alexandria — important for Oriental Orthodox history and Chalcedonian controversy, but I did not find a strong Audible biography.
* Watchlist — St. Gregory the Illuminator, St. Nino, Armenian and Georgian saints — I did not find strong Audible saint biographies in this pass.

8. Group / Miracle / Devotional Collections — Use Only as Supplements

These expand the number of saints represented, but they are not your main historical-critical lane.

* P3 — Broad saints survey — Stories of the Saints by Carey Wallace. More accessible / illustrated-entry style; useful for breadth, not critical scholarship.  ￼
* P4 — Miracle-focused collection — Saints Who Raised the Dead. Includes St. Francis Xavier, St. Patrick, St. John Bosco, St. Catherine of Siena, St. Teresa of Ávila, St. Elizabeth of Hungary, St. Rose of Lima, and others, but the framing is miracle/devotional rather than historical-critical.  ￼
* P4 — Vision / hell tradition collection — Saints Who Saw Hell. Includes John Bosco, Faustina, Teresa of Ávila, and others; useful only for studying Catholic visionary tradition or devotional reception.  ￼
* P4 — Children’s / introductory collection — Catholic Saints for Kids. Broad representation, but not suitable for your historical-critical target.  ￼

Filter used

I kept this Audible-first, with historical-critical / scholarly / serious biography ahead of devotional hagiography. I also omitted exact titles already listed earlier and avoided direct biblical figures. When Audible has only primary sources, hagiography, Virtual Voice, or short devotional material, I marked the item lower priority.

Priority key:
P1 = strongest fit: serious biography / scholarly history / critical historical method
P2 = good fit, but narrower, more sympathetic, or less critical
P3 = useful primary source, lecture, or context item
P4 = defer: devotional, hagiographic, Virtual Voice, children’s/short, or non-Audible watchlist

6. Pre-Christian / Israelite-Adjacent Extra-Biblical Figures

This category is thinner because ancient Israelite and Near Eastern “holy person” categories do not map neatly onto later saints/rabbis. The closest Audible material is usually primary texts, Hellenistic Jewish history, Persian rulers, Maccabees/Hasmoneans, priests, scribes, and sages.

* P2 — Maccabean martyrs / Judas Maccabeus / Hasmonean revolt — The Books of I & II Maccabees — primary source audio; important for Jewish martyrdom, temple restoration, priestly politics, and later Christian saint/martyr traditions. — [Audible](https://www.audible.com/pd/The-Books-of-I-II-Maccabees-Audiobook/B01M1A0FOF)
* P3 — Judah Maccabee — Judah the Maccabee — very short audio; useful only as a quick supplement, not critical history. — [Audible](https://www.audible.com/pd/Judah-the-Maccabee-Audiobook/B002V57NUM)
* P3 — Cyrus the Great — Xenophon, Cyrus the Great: The Arts of Leadership and War — ancient idealized portrait of Cyrus, relevant to Jewish/Persian context, but not modern critical biography. — [Audible](https://www.audible.com/pd/Xenophons-Cyrus-the-Great-Audiobook/B07C4HW7DH)
* P4 — Cyrus / Persian rulers — Virtual Voice leadership adaptations — defer; not historical-critical.
* P4 — Ancient Assyrian / Babylonian “holy people” — I would not inflate this category. In Assyrian and Mesopotamian contexts the closer equivalents are kings, priests, sages, scribes, diviners, and gods, not saints. Use the ANE / Assyriology titles from the earlier list rather than weak individual “holy person” biographies.
* P4 — Canaanite / Levantine religious figures and deities — treat as religious-history context, not saint biography. Baal/Hadad and related figures belong under ancient Near Eastern religion rather than holy-person biography.

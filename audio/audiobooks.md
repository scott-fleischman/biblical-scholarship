## Best expanded starting order

For your stated interests, I would start in this order:

1. **Kugel — *How to Read the Bible***
2. **Barton — *A History of the Bible***
3. **Schmid and Schröter — *The Making of the Bible***
4. **Friedman — *Who Wrote the Bible?***
5. **Wright — *Why the Bible Began***
6. **Coogan — *The Old Testament***
7. **Finkelstein and Silberman — *The Bible Unearthed***
8. **Law — *When God Spoke Greek***
9. **Collins or Lim — Dead Sea Scrolls titles**
10. **Fredriksen — *When Christians Were Jews***
11. **Fredriksen — *Paul: The Pagans’ Apostle***
12. **Levine and Brettler — *The Bible With and Without Jesus***
13. **Ehrman — *Jesus, Interrupted* / *Lost Scriptures* / *Jesus Before the Gospels***
14. **Pagels — *The Gnostic Gospels* / *The Origin of Satan***
15. **Litwa — *How the Gospels Became History***
16. **Moss — *God’s Ghostwriters***
17. **Magness — *Masada***
18. **Goodman — *A History of Judaism***
19. **Podany — *Weavers, Scribes, and Kings***
20. **Frahm — *Assyria***

That gives you a much fuller Audible-based historical-critical curriculum: Hebrew Bible formation, Pentateuchal criticism, archaeology, Septuagint, Second Temple Judaism, Dead Sea Scrolls, historical Jesus, Paul, early Christian diversity, rabbinic transition, and ANE / Assyriology background.

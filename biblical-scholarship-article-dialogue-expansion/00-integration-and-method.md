# 00. Integration and Method

## Purpose

This expansion supplies the article-dialogue layer missing from most bibliography projects. It is organized around directed scholarly conversations rather than flat lists.

A flat bibliography says:

> Here are important works on Paul.

A dialogue bibliography says:

> Stendahl critiques the introspective Western Paul; Sanders changes the Judaism baseline; Dunn names the New Perspective; Nanos, Eisenbaum, and Fredriksen move the discussion toward Paul within Judaism; Barclay reframes grace through gift theory; recent state-of-the-question work asks how social history changes the model.

## Edge types

Use these edge types consistently:

- `responds`: direct response, review essay, rebuttal, or symposium contribution.
- `challenges`: rejects a claim or model.
- `critiques`: critiques method, assumptions, or framing.
- `extends`: builds on an earlier work while adding material, texts, methods, or contexts.
- `revises`: changes the model while preserving part of it.
- `reframes`: changes the question.
- `revives`: returns to an older model in a new form.
- `retrospective`: later reflection by the same scholar or debate participants.
- `parallel`: another debate that should be read beside the first.
- `ethical corrective`: provenance, forgery, coloniality, or evidentiary-warning intervention.

## Verification rules

1. Do not import a node into a formal Zotero/BibTeX file until page numbers, DOI, title punctuation, journal issue, and original-language publication history are verified.
2. Every new article must have at least one edge.
3. Current articles should be traced backward at least three levels when possible:
   - immediate interlocutors,
   - methodological predecessors,
   - older disciplinary ancestors.
4. Do not treat monographs as substitutes for articles. Monographs appear here only when they are necessary waypoints in article dialogue.
5. For every field, include at least one critical-method, non-English, material-culture, or reception-history path when available.

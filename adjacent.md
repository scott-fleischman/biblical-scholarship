Below is a **map of adjacent histories and adjacent religions** that materially affect biblical studies, Second Temple Judaism, Christian origins, reception history, and “holy life” traditions. This is not another list of biblical commentaries. It is the **world around the Bible**: political history, religions, ritual systems, texts, priests, sages, philosophers, holy men, ascetics, scribes, diviners, and interpreters.

I organize each sphere by:

**Why it matters** — what it changes for biblical/Jewish/Christian study.
**Primary texts** — the texts to read.
**Secondary scholarship** — the modern research shelf.
**Holy/devoted life** — priests, sages, ascetics, holy people, ritual experts, or exemplars.
**Non-English / technical controls** — works and editions that experts often use.

# 2. Mesopotamia: Sumerian, Akkadian, Babylonian, Assyrian

## Why it matters

Mesopotamia is the main comparative world for **creation, flood, law, kingship, prophecy, divination, lament, wisdom, exile, empire, temple ideology, scribal education, covenant/treaty forms, demons, angels, calendars, and apocalyptic precursors**. Biblical scholars who do not know Mesopotamian texts often misread Genesis, law, prophetic signs, royal ideology, and apocalyptic symbolism.

## Primary texts

### Myth, epic, and primeval traditions

* **[E] *Epic of Gilgamesh*** — flood tradition, mortality, kingship, friendship, wisdom.
* **[E] *Atrahasis*** — creation of humanity, flood, divine-human conflict.
* **[E] *Enūma Eliš*** — Babylonian creation and Marduk theology.
* **[E] *Erra and Ishum*** — divine violence, plague, cosmic disorder.
* **[E] Sumerian flood and creation traditions** — especially through ETCSL.

### Law, treaties, and statecraft

* **[E] Laws of Hammurabi**
* **[E] Laws of Eshnunna**
* **[E] Laws of Lipit-Ishtar**
* **[E] Middle Assyrian Laws**
* **[E] Neo-Assyrian treaties, especially Esarhaddon’s Succession Treaty** — indispensable for covenant/treaty comparison with Deuteronomy.
* **[E] State Archives of Assyria royal correspondence, prophecies, rituals, treaties**

### Wisdom, lament, prayer, and piety

* **[E] *Ludlul bēl nēmeqi* / “I Will Praise the Lord of Wisdom”** — often compared to Job.
* **[E] *Babylonian Theodicy*** — dialogue on justice and suffering.
* **[E] Sumerian city laments** — essential for Lamentations and Zion theology comparisons.
* **[E] Akkadian prayers to Marduk, Ishtar, Shamash**
* **[E] Šurpu and Maqlû incantation series** — sin, impurity, witchcraft, exorcism.
* **[E] Namburbi rituals** — averting evil portents.

### Divination and scholarly religion

* **[E] *Enūma Anu Enlil*** — celestial divination.
* **[E] *Bārûtu*** — extispicy.
* **[E] Diagnostic Handbook / Sakikkû**
* **[E] Omen series, dream books, hemerologies, ritual calendars**
* **[E] Apkallu traditions** — antediluvian sages; important for Enochic and Watchers comparisons.

## Primary-source access

* **[E] Benjamin Foster, *Before the Muses: An Anthology of Akkadian Literature*** — the major English Akkadian literary anthology; the 3rd edition is described as a comprehensive collection spanning three millennia, with new translations, fragments, notes, and updated commentary. ([Eisenbrauns][8])
* **[E] SAA / State Archives of Assyria** — critical text editions of Neo-Assyrian archive texts. ([Assyriologia][7])

* **[E] SBL *Writings from the Ancient World*** volumes on Mesopotamian texts, laws, rituals, letters, inscriptions. ([SBL Site][4])

* **[E] Jean Bottéro, *Religion in Ancient Mesopotamia*** — essential for gods, ritual, divination, and piety.

* **[E] W. G. Lambert, *Babylonian Creation Myths*** — essential for *Enūma Eliš* and creation mythology.

* **[E] Alan Lenzi, *Secrecy and the Gods*** — divine secrets, scribal knowledge, and learned religion.
* **[E] Amar Annus, Mesopotamian myth, apkallu, and biblical-adjacent traditions**.

## Holy/devoted life

Mesopotamia does not have “saints” in the Jewish/Christian sense, but it has **ritual experts and sages**:

* **āšipu** — exorcist-healers; relevant to demons, impurity, healing, and ritual power.
* **bārû** — diviner; relevant to signs, omens, and divine communication.
* **kalû** — lamentation priest; relevant to temple lament, communal grief, and ritual performance.
* **ṭupšarru** — scholar-scribe; relevant to revelation, hidden knowledge, and learned piety.
* **apkallu** — mythic sages; relevant to Enochic/Watchers traditions.

Read:

* **[E] Alan Lenzi, *Secrecy and the Gods***
* **[E] Francesca Rochberg, *The Heavenly Writing***
* **[I] Jean Bottéro, divination and religion essays**
* **[I] Erica Reiner, *Astral Magic in Babylonia***
* **[I] Stefan Maul, *Zukunftsbewältigung* [German]** — divination and coping with the future.

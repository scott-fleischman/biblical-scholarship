# 06. Versional and Textual-Critical Research Ecosystem

## Why this file is needed

A bibliography that names BHS/BHQ, Göttingen Septuagint, NA/UBS, ECM, DJD, and a few textbook authors has a good start. Specialists expect a fuller ecosystem: manuscripts, editions, apparatuses, paratexts, versions, daughter translations, lectionaries, patristic citations, masorah, scribal habits, stemmatics, CBGM, social textual criticism, and book-by-book textual histories.

## General textual-critical principles

1. **Separate edition, manuscript, version, and commentary.** A critical edition is not a manuscript; a translation is not a textual witness unless its Vorlage and translation technique are assessed.
2. **Do not treat “the Hebrew” or “the Greek” as singular.** Biblical texts often circulated in pluriform textual traditions.
3. **Record the edition used.** BHS, BHQ, HUBP, Göttingen, Rahlfs-Hanhart, NETS, NA28, UBS5, ECM, and other editions differ in text and apparatus.
4. **Add versional specialists by book.** LXX Genesis scholarship differs from LXX Jeremiah, LXX Daniel, or LXX Psalms.
5. **Use manuscripts and paratexts.** Marginalia, masorah, nomina sacra, capitula, lectionary marks, corrections, and catenae can be evidence.
6. **Tag text-critical claims by evidence type:** MT, DSS, SP, LXX, Targum, Peshitta, Vetus Latina, Vulgate, Coptic, Armenian, Georgian, Ethiopic, Arabic, Slavonic, patristic citation, lectionary.

## Patristic citations and catenae

A research ecosystem should include patristic citations as textual evidence and reception evidence.

Add:

- Greek catenae.
- Latin commentaries.
- Syriac scholia.
- Coptic and Armenian citations.
- Origen, Eusebius, Didymus, Chrysostom, Augustine, Jerome, Theodoret, Cyril, Ephrem, Aphrahat.
- Methodological warnings: citations may be paraphrased, harmonized, translated, memory-based, or altered by later copyists.

## Minimum specialist-satisfaction checklist

For every biblical book, add:

- Main critical text(s).
- Key manuscript witnesses.
- Versional witnesses with book-specific bibliography.
- Known textual cruxes.
- Representative text-critical articles.
- Recent edition/revision status.
- Digital manuscript access points.
- Quality note on when versions may or may not reconstruct Hebrew/Greek Vorlagen.

# 13. Reception, Liturgy, Preaching, and Lived Religion

## Why this file is needed

The Bible is not only read in commentaries. It is sung, prayed, preached, memorized, translated, staged, painted, legislated, weaponized, worn, digitized, and ritualized. A research ecosystem must represent reception as practice, not only as intellectual history.

## Scope

- Jewish, Christian, Islamic, secular, and popular reception.
- Liturgy, lectionaries, prayer, hymnody, piyyut, chant.
- Sermons, preaching, homiletics, postils, revival, Black preaching, liberation preaching.
- Art, iconography, manuscripts, architecture, film, music, theater, comics, internet culture.
- Bible translation, missions, colonialism, education, children’s Bibles.
- Bible in law, politics, nationalism, race, gender, sexuality, science, medicine, and public controversies.
- Ethnographic and lived-religion approaches.

## Quality-control warnings

1. **Reception is not secondary to “real meaning.”** Reception creates social, political, ritual, and material meanings.
2. **Do not restrict reception to elite commentary.** Sermons, songs, images, rituals, schoolbooks, and social media matter.
3. **Do not treat popular reception as unscholarly.** Use rigorous methods from anthropology, media studies, art history, musicology, and sociology.
4. **Do not flatten communities.** Jewish, Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant, Muslim, secular, African, Asian, Latin American, Indigenous, and diasporic receptions differ internally.
5. **Flag harmful reception.** Slavery, antisemitism, anti-Judaism, colonialism, racism, misogyny, homophobia, and nationalism should be named, not hidden.
6. **Use primary reception artifacts.** Do not rely only on modern summaries of reception history.

# 04. Historical Jesus, Gospel Memory, and the Synoptic Problem

## Why this file is needed

A biblical-studies bibliography that has Gospel commentaries but lacks a dedicated Historical Jesus and Synoptic Problem file will look incomplete to many New Testament specialists. These debates are not optional side topics; they shape source criticism, Gospel dating, Jesus research, oral tradition, memory studies, Christology, early Judaism, and Christian origins.

## Scope

This file covers:

- Historical Jesus research.
- Criteria and critiques of criteria.
- Gospel memory, orality, and performance.
- Synoptic Problem models.
- Q scholarship.
- Farrer/Mark without Q.
- Griesbach/Two-Gospel and Matthean posteriority.
- Marcion and Gospel relationship debates.
- Gospel genre, book production, and circulation.
- Jewish, Galilean, Roman, and material contexts for Jesus.

## Primary sources and control texts

### Canonical Gospels and Acts

- Critical Greek text: NA28, UBS5, ECM where available, and major apparatuses.
- Synopses: Kurt Aland, Huck-Greeven, Throckmorton, Orchard, and digital synopsis tools.
- Parallel Gospel tools: English and Greek synopses; caution that harmonies are not neutral.
- Textual witnesses: P45, P66, P75, major majuscules, patristic citations, early translations.

### Jewish and Greco-Roman comparanda

- Josephus, especially *Jewish War*, *Antiquities*, and *Life*.
- Philo for Hellenistic Jewish exegesis, ethics, and diaspora contexts.
- Dead Sea Scrolls for sectarian law, eschatology, purity, messianism, apocalypticism, and scriptural interpretation.
- Early rabbinic traditions, used cautiously and historically.
- Pseudepigrapha and apocrypha: 1 Enoch, Jubilees, Psalms of Solomon, 4 Ezra, 2 Baruch, Testament traditions, Wisdom, Sirach.
- Greco-Roman moralists, biographers, historiographers, rhetoricians, novelists, inscriptions, papyri, and association evidence.

### Material culture

- Galilean archaeology: Sepphoris, Capernaum, Magdala, Nazareth, Bethsaida, Chorazin, rural Galilee, synagogues, stone vessels, miqva'ot.
- Judean and Jerusalem archaeology: Temple Mount contexts, ritual baths, ossuaries, tombs, Pilate inscription, Caiaphas ossuary debates, coins, roads.
- Roman administration and crucifixion evidence: inscriptions, legal texts, Josephus, Philo, Tacitus, archaeological evidence such as crucifixion remains.

### Two-Source / Four-Source Hypothesis

**Core claim:** Matthew and Luke used Mark independently, and both also used Q or a sayings source/tradition.

**Expected names and works:**

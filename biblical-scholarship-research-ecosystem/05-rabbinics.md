# 05. Rabbinics as an Independent Field

## Why this file is needed

Biblical bibliographies often treat rabbinic literature as “reception” of the Bible. That is inadequate. Rabbinic literature is also an independent legal, literary, ritual, scholastic, social, linguistic, and manuscript tradition. Biblical scholars need rabbinics for interpretation history, Jewish law, late antique Judaism, targum, midrash, canon, liturgy, pedagogy, and reception; rabbinics specialists expect the field to be represented on its own terms.

## Scope

This file covers:

- Tannaitic literature.
- Amoraic literature.
- Midrash halakhah and midrash aggadah.
- Targum and Aramaic.
- Piyyut and synagogue liturgy.
- Hekhalot, merkabah, magic, amulets, and adjuration traditions.
- Geniza evidence.
- Medieval rabbinic commentary and legal tradition as continuation and reception.
- Rabbinic method: redaction, oral/written transmission, scholasticism, attribution, manuscript variation, intertextuality, legal reasoning.

## Primary corpora and critical editions

### Mishnah and Tosefta

- Mishnah: Kaufmann manuscript, Parma manuscript, Cambridge Lowe manuscript, printed editions, Albeck, Blackman, Danby, Sefaria for access, and specialized critical work by tractate.
- Tosefta: Zuckermandel edition, Lieberman’s *Tosefta ki-Fshutah*, Saul Lieberman’s commentaries, Vienna and Erfurt manuscripts.
- Tractate-specific scholarship should be added for any biblical topic involving law, ritual, purity, Sabbath, festivals, marriage, damages, sacrifice, or temple.

### Talmuds

- Palestinian Talmud / Yerushalmi: Venice edition, Leiden manuscript, Academy of the Hebrew Language materials, Sussmann, Schäfer/Synopse where relevant.

- Translations: Soncino, Neusner, Steinsaltz/Koren, Sefaria, William Davidson Talmud; these are access points, not replacements for manuscript-critical work.

## Article-level debate clusters

### The Mishnah: code, curriculum, oral tradition, or scholastic text?

**Questions:** Is Mishnah a law code, educational text, ritual taxonomy, oral performance script, philosophical-legal project, or elite scholastic artifact?

**Search terms:** `Mishnah law code`, `Mishnah oral tradition`, `Mishnah pedagogy`, `tannaitic redaction`, `Mishnah ritual`, `Mishnah subject formation`.

**Expected scholars:** Neusner, Hauptman, Alexander, Rosen-Zvi, Balberg, Hayes, Berkowitz, Yadin-Israel, Fraade.

### Bavli formation and the stammaim

**Questions:** What layers are recoverable? How do anonymous editorial voices reshape earlier traditions? How does the Bavli compare with Palestinian rabbinic material?

**Search terms:** `stammaim`, `Bavli redaction`, `talmudic sugya source criticism`, `Bavli scholasticism`, `anonymous voice Talmud`.

**Expected scholars:** Halivni, Friedman, Rubenstein, Kalmin, Vidas, Gray, Bar-Asher Siegal, Elman, Gafni.

### Rabbinic biblical interpretation and midrash

**Questions:** How do rabbis read Scripture? What is the relation between peshat, derash, halakhic midrash, aggadic midrash, intertextuality, and performance?

**Search terms:** `rabbinic exegesis`, `midrash intertextuality`, `halakhic midrash`, `aggadic midrash`, `rabbinic hermeneutics`, `petihta`, `mashal`.

**Expected scholars:** Kugel, Fishbane, Boyarin, Stern, Fraade, Hirshman, Yadin-Israel, Bakhos, Teugels.

### Rabbinics and early Christianity

**Questions:** How should rabbinic and Christian texts be compared? What counts as shared late antique culture? How do polemic, proximity, and parallel development work?

**Search terms:** `rabbis and Christians`, `late antique Judaism Christianity`, `rabbinic Christian polemic`, `Bavli and Syriac Christianity`, `parting of the ways`.

**Expected scholars:** Daniel Boyarin, Israel Yuval, Peter Schäfer, Michal Bar-Asher Siegal, Adam Becker, Annette Reed, Paula Fredriksen, Shaye Cohen, Seth Schwartz, Tessa Rajak.

### Rabbinic law and Roman/Sasanian law

**Questions:** How does rabbinic law relate to imperial legal cultures? Are similarities due to influence, shared social problems, or comparative legal reasoning?

**Search terms:** `rabbinic law Roman law`, `rabbinic law Sasanian law`, `legal pluralism rabbis`, `tannaitic law empire`.

**Expected scholars:** Beth Berkowitz, Catherine Hezser, Yair Furstenberg, Christine Hayes, Richard Kalmin, Geoffrey Herman, Maria Macuch for Sasanian context.

### Gender, sexuality, and body

**Questions:** How do rabbinic texts construct gendered bodies, sexual norms, reproduction, menstruation, marriage, divorce, and family?

**Search terms:** `rabbinic gender`, `niddah body`, `rabbinic sexuality`, `rabbinic marriage`, `rabbinic masculinity`, `queer rabbinics`.

**Expected scholars:** Fonrobert, Hauptman, Labovitz, Rosen-Zvi, Balberg, Ilan, Kessler, Lev, Weisberg, Boyarin.

### Piyyut, synagogue, and liturgical Bible

**Questions:** How does Scripture function in synagogue poetry, lectionary cycles, prayer, and festival performance?

**Search terms:** `piyyut biblical interpretation`, `Yannai`, `Qallir`, `lectionary cycle`, `Geniza liturgy`, `synagogue poetry Bible`.

**Expected scholars:** Fleischer, Elizur, Yahalom, Swartz, Lieber, Münz-Manor, Langer.

## Quality-control warnings

1. **Do not cite rabbinic texts as direct evidence for first-century Jesus or Paul without argument.** Date, redaction, geography, and genre matter.
2. **Do not treat printed Vilna Bavli as the stable ancient text.** Manuscripts and Geniza fragments may matter.
3. **Do not flatten tannaitic, amoraic, Palestinian, Babylonian, and medieval layers.**
4. **Do not treat aggadah as “mere folklore” or halakhah as “mere law.”** Genre and performance matter.
5. **Do not use Sefaria or translations as critical editions.** They are access tools.
6. **Mark Neusner carefully.** Foundational and productive, but many claims and translations are contested.
7. **Include Hebrew scholarship.** A rabbinics bibliography without Hebrew-language scholarship will look severely incomplete.

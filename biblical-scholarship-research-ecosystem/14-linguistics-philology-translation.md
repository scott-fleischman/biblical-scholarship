# 14. Linguistics, Philology, and Translation Studies

## Why this file is needed

The repository already has a broad linguistics file. This supplement sharpens the research ecosystem: it connects language study to corpora, grammars, lexica, textual criticism, translation theory, sociolinguistics, discourse analysis, and digital philology. Biblical experts expect language tools to be grouped by language, period, corpus, and method.

## General principles

1. **Separate grammar, lexicon, corpus, and method.** A grammar is not a corpus; a lexicon is not a semantic argument.
2. **Record language stage.** Biblical Hebrew, Qumran Hebrew, Mishnaic Hebrew, Jewish Babylonian Aramaic, Syriac, Koine Greek, Coptic, and Ge'ez are not interchangeable.
3. **Use corpus data with edition awareness.** Tagged corpora inherit decisions about tokenization, morphology, syntax, and textual base.
4. **Do not overclaim from etymology.** Semantics depends on usage, context, register, and discourse.
5. **Treat translation as interpretation.** Ancient versions and modern translations need translation-theory controls.

### Quality-control warnings

1. **Do not cite lexica as arguments.** Lexica summarize evidence; arguments must analyze usage.
2. **Do not use etymology as meaning.** Etymology can inform but not determine synchronic meaning.
3. **Do not use theological dictionaries uncritically.** They often organize meanings by doctrinal interests.
4. **Do not flatten language stages.** Biblical Hebrew, Qumran Hebrew, Mishnaic Hebrew, and Modern Hebrew differ.
5. **Do not infer a Hebrew Vorlage from a version without translation-technique analysis.**
6. **Do not overread hapax legomena.** Use comparative evidence cautiously.
7. **Do not ignore manuscript variation in linguistic analysis.**
8. **Do not assume a modern punctuation or versification system is ancient.**

# 15. Pedagogy, Public Bible, Museums, and Public Scholarship

## Why this file is needed

SBL includes teachers, museum professionals, public scholars, digital educators, translators, clergy-adjacent scholars, and researchers of Bible in public life. A research ecosystem should include how biblical scholarship is taught, mediated, politicized, displayed, and misunderstood outside specialist monographs.

## Scope

- Teaching biblical studies in universities, seminaries, religious schools, public schools, prisons, churches, synagogues, and online spaces.
- Public Bible controversies.
- Bible museums and exhibitions.
- Digital pedagogy.
- Open educational resources.
- Religious literacy.
- Bible and law/politics.
- Public scholarship ethics.
- Pedagogy for difficult texts: violence, slavery, rape, anti-Judaism, racism, colonialism, sexuality, disability, and trauma.

## Quality-control warnings

1. **Do not confuse public-facing with low quality.** Some public scholarship is rigorous; some academic prose is poor.
2. **Do not cite devotional resources as academic authorities without labeling them.**
3. **Do not present difficult texts without reception history.** Harm often comes from later uses.
4. **Do not assume students share religious literacy.** Explain terms.
5. **Do not assume students are secular.** Academic teaching can be critical without contempt for religious practice.
6. **Do not use museum artifacts without provenance notes.**
7. **Do not use digital tools without explaining editions, data, and limits.**

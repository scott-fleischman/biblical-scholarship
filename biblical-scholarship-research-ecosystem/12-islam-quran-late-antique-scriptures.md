# 12. Islam, Qur'an, and Late Antique Scriptural Worlds

## Why this file is needed

Biblical scholarship often treats Islam and the Qur'an as distant reception history. That is too narrow. The Qur'an, tafsir, Syriac Christianity, Christian Arabic, Jewish Arabic, late antique homiletic and liturgical traditions, and Muslim-Jewish-Christian polemics are major parts of the Bible's interpretive afterlife and of late antique scriptural culture.

## Scope

- Qur'anic engagement with biblical figures and traditions.
- Tafsir and isra'iliyyat.
- Tahrif and debates over scriptural corruption.
- Syriac Christian and Jewish traditions in late antiquity.

- Jewish Arabic Bible translations and exegesis.

- Bible in Islamicate societies.
- Arabic, Persian, Ethiopic, Syriac, Coptic, Armenian, and Georgian reception of biblical traditions.

## Article-level debate clusters

### Qur'an and the Bible: influence, conversation, or late antique discourse?

**Questions:** Does the Qur'an depend on written biblical texts, oral traditions, liturgical/homiletic traditions, Syriac Christian materials, Jewish midrash, or a broader late antique scriptural discourse?

**Search terms:** `Qur'an Bible`, `Qur'an biblical tradition`, `Qur'an late antiquity`, `Qur'an Syriac`, `Qur'an midrash`, `Qur'an apocrypha`.

**Expected scholars:** Neuwirth, Reynolds, Griffith, Sinai, Pregill, Witztum, Zellentin, El-Badawi, Shoemaker, McAuliffe.

### Tahrif and scriptural corruption

**Questions:** How did Muslim writers understand Jewish and Christian scriptures? Did tahrif mean textual corruption, interpretive distortion, concealment, or all of these in different contexts?

**Search terms:** `tahrif Bible`, `scriptural corruption Islam`, `Ibn Hazm Bible`, `Muslim polemic scripture`, `Tawrat Injil Qur'an`.

**Expected scholars:** Camilla Adang, Gabriel Said Reynolds, Martin Accad, David Thomas, Sidney Griffith, Sabine Schmidtke, Mun'im Sirry.

### Mary and Jesus in Qur'an and Christian apocrypha

**Questions:** How do Qur'anic Mary and Jesus traditions relate to Protevangelium of James, infancy traditions, Syriac homilies, and liturgical materials?

**Search terms:** `Mary Qur'an Protevangelium`, `Jesus Qur'an infancy gospel`, `Maryam Qur'an Syriac`, `Qur'an crucifixion Jesus`.

**Expected scholars:** Griffith, Reynolds, Shoemaker, Horn, Pregill, Witztum, Neuwirth, McAuliffe.

### Abraham, sacrifice, and monotheism

**Questions:** How do Jewish, Christian, and Muslim traditions construe Abraham, sacrifice, covenant, election, and anti-idolatry?

**Search terms:** `Abraham Qur'an Bible`, `sacrifice son Qur'an`, `Ibrahim hanif`, `Abraham interreligious polemic`.

**Expected scholars:** Firestone, Reynolds, Levenson for Jewish/Christian sacrifice background, Tottoli, Wheeler, Neuwirth.

### Moses and the Exodus in Islam

**Questions:** How do Qur'anic and tafsir traditions reinterpret Moses, Pharaoh, signs, law, and liberation?

**Search terms:** `Moses Qur'an Exodus`, `Pharaoh Qur'an`, `Musa tafsir`, `Qur'an Sinai`, `Qur'an Israelites`.

**Expected scholars:** Wheeler, Reynolds, Pregill, Neuwirth, El-Badawi, Sinai.

### Christian Arabic Bible translation

**Questions:** How did Arabic-speaking Christians translate, interpret, and defend biblical texts under Islamic rule? How do Arabic versions relate to Greek, Syriac, Coptic, and Latin Vorlagen?

**Search terms:** `Arabic Bible manuscripts`, `Christian Arabic Gospel`, `Arabic Psalter`, `Sinai Arabic Bible`, `Arabic Bible translation medieval`.

**Expected scholars:** Kashouh, Vollandt, Polliack, Griffith, Swanson, Monferrer-Sala, Treiger.

### Jewish Arabic exegesis and Saadia

**Questions:** How did Jewish exegetes in Arabic negotiate rabbinic tradition, grammar, philosophy, kalam, Karaite critique, and Muslim polemics?

**Search terms:** `Saadia Bible translation`, `Judeo-Arabic exegesis`, `Karaite Arabic commentary`, `Jewish Arabic Bible`.

**Expected scholars:** Polliack, Ben-Shammai, Stroumsa, Frank, Zawanowska, Adang, Schmidtke.

## Quality-control warnings

1. **Do not assume the Qur'an simply copies the Bible.** Scholarship increasingly frames the Qur'an within a broad late antique scriptural and oral-literate environment.
2. **Do not treat “the Bible” in Islamic polemic as one stable object.** Tawrat, Zabur, Injil, Jewish scriptures, Christian Gospels, lectionary traditions, and actual manuscripts may differ.
3. **Do not use tafsir without date and school controls.** Early, classical, medieval, modern, Sunni, Shi'i, rationalist, mystical, and legal tafsir differ.
4. **Do not ignore Syriac Christianity.** It is often essential for late antique biblical tradition.
5. **Do not treat Arabic Bible translations as mere derivatives.** They are major textual and reception witnesses.
6. **Distinguish polemic from description.** Muslim, Christian, and Jewish authors often present rival scriptures strategically.
7. **Include Arabic, German, French, and Hebrew scholarship.** Anglophone-only coverage will be incomplete.

# 10. Early Judaism, Qumran, and Second Temple Literature

## Why this file is needed

Hebrew Bible and New Testament scholarship both depend on early Jewish literature, but bibliographies often scatter it across apocrypha, pseudepigrapha, Qumran, Josephus, Philo, and rabbinics. This file treats Second Temple Judaism and Qumran as an integrated research ecosystem with its own primary corpora, journals, article debates, and methods.

## Scope

- Persian, Hellenistic, Hasmonean, Herodian, and early Roman Jewish history.
- Apocrypha/Deuterocanon and Pseudepigrapha.
- Dead Sea Scrolls and Judaean Desert texts.
- Philo and Hellenistic Judaism.
- Josephus.
- Diaspora Judaism.
- Temple, priesthood, law, purity, apocalypticism, wisdom, rewritten scripture, prayer, liturgy, magic, and material culture.
- Jewish groups: Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, scribes, priests, common people, diaspora communities.

## Article-level debate clusters

### What counted as Scripture?

**Questions:** How did scriptural authority function before a fixed canon? How did citation, interpretation, rewriting, and collection practices mark authority?

**Search terms:** `scriptural authority Qumran`, `canon Second Temple Judaism`, `rewritten scripture`, `authoritative texts`, `Bible at Qumran`.

**Expected scholars:** Eugene Ulrich, John Collins, Armin Lange, Hindy Najman, Molly Zahn, Michael Segal, Sidnie White Crawford, George Brooke.

### Rewritten Scripture and inner-biblical interpretation

**Questions:** Is “rewritten Bible” a genre, a scribal technique, or a modern category? How do Jubilees, Genesis Apocryphon, Temple Scroll, and Reworked Pentateuch relate to biblical texts?

**Search terms:** `rewritten Bible genre`, `rewritten scripture Qumran`, `Jubilees Genesis`, `Temple Scroll Deuteronomy`, `Reworked Pentateuch`.

**Expected scholars:** Geza Vermes as classic background; Sidnie White Crawford, Molly Zahn, Michael Segal, Hindy Najman, Moshe Bernstein, James Kugel.

### Sectarianism and the yahad

**Questions:** How should the Qumran movement be related to Essenes, sectarian identity, social boundaries, and the archaeological site?

**Search terms:** `Qumran sectarianism`, `yahad`, `Essenes Qumran`, `Community Rule`, `Damascus Document`, `Teacher of Righteousness`.

**Expected scholars:** John Collins, Eyal Regev, Charlotte Hempel, Sarianna Metso, Carol Newsom, Maxine Grossman, Jodi Magness, Joan Taylor, Lawrence Schiffman.

### Qumran archaeology and settlement

**Questions:** Was Qumran a sectarian settlement, villa, fortress, pottery production site, or something else? How do caves relate to the site?

**Search terms:** `Qumran archaeology`, `Qumran cemetery`, `Qumran caves site`, `Qumran sectarian settlement`, `Judaean Desert caves`.

**Expected scholars:** Roland de Vaux as classic background; Jodi Magness, Yizhar Hirschfeld, Rachel Hachlili, Hanan Eshel, Joan Taylor, Eric Meyers, Dennis Mizzi.

### Law, purity, and calendar

**Questions:** How do Qumran law and calendar distinguish the movement? How do they relate to priestly, biblical, and rabbinic law?

**Search terms:** `4QMMT`, `Qumran halakhah`, `Qumran purity`, `Qumran calendar`, `Temple Scroll law`, `Damascus Document law`.

**Expected scholars:** Lawrence Schiffman, Joseph Baumgarten, Aharon Shemesh, Vered Noam, Cana Werman, Jonathan Klawans, Charlotte Hempel.

### Apocalypticism and empire

**Questions:** How do apocalyptic texts respond to empire, persecution, cosmology, angels, eschatology, and theodicy?

**Search terms:** `apocalypticism empire`, `Daniel apocalypse`, `1 Enoch Watchers`, `4 Ezra trauma`, `2 Baruch`, `apocalyptic genre`.

**Expected scholars:** John Collins, Paul Hanson, Martha Himmelfarb, Michael Stone, Loren Stuckenbruck, Anathea Portier-Young, Hindy Najman.

### Philo and Hellenistic Jewish philosophy

**Questions:** How does Philo negotiate Torah, Platonism, Stoicism, allegory, politics, diaspora, and imperial power?

**Search terms:** `Philo allegory`, `Philo Torah philosophy`, `Philo politics`, `Philo Embassy Gaius`, `Philo Logos`, `Hellenistic Judaism Alexandria`.

**Expected scholars:** David Winston, Peder Borgen, Gregory Sterling, David Runia, Maren Niehoff, Ellen Birnbaum, Sarah Pearce, Carlos Lévy.

### Josephus and historiography

**Questions:** How does Josephus construct Jewish history, Roman power, priestly identity, Pharisees/Sadducees/Essenes, revolt, and apologetic identity?

**Search terms:** `Josephus historiography`, `Josephus Pharisees`, `Josephus Essenes`, `Josephus Jewish War`, `Against Apion`, `Josephus Rome`.

**Expected scholars:** Steve Mason, Tessa Rajak, Louis Feldman, Daniel Schwartz, Jonathan Price, Martin Goodman, Seth Schwartz, Honora Chapman.

### Gender and women in early Judaism

**Questions:** How do early Jewish texts represent women, gendered bodies, marriage, sexuality, purity, widows, mothers, queens, heroines, and female exemplars?

**Search terms:** `women Qumran`, `gender Second Temple Judaism`, `Judith gender`, `Susanna`, `Tobit marriage`, `Ben Sira women`, `Philo women`.

**Expected scholars:** Tal Ilan, Ross Kraemer, Judith Hauptman, Maxine Grossman, Eileen Schuller, Charlotte Hempel, Bernadette Brooten, Adele Reinhartz.

## Quality-control warnings

1. **Do not use “intertestamental” as a neutral field label.** Use “Second Temple,” “early Judaism,” or corpus-specific labels.
2. **Do not treat Qumran as normative Judaism.** It is one or more particular communities/textual collections.
3. **Do not assume Pharisees equal rabbis.** Continuity and discontinuity need argument.
4. **Do not read later rabbinic law directly into the first century without dating and genre controls.**
5. **Do not treat Josephus as transparent reportage.** His rhetoric, audience, and Roman context matter.
6. **Do not treat “apocalyptic” as a synonym for bizarre.** Use genre, worldview, social setting, and rhetoric carefully.
7. **Use provenance controls for Judaean Desert artifacts and post-2002 fragments.**

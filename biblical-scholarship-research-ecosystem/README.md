# Biblical Scholarship Research Ecosystem Expansion

This package is a drop-in expansion for `scott-fleischman/biblical-scholarship`. It does not replace the existing book lists. It adds the missing research infrastructure that specialists usually expect: article-level discovery, journals and indexes, primary corpora, textual-versional control, rabbinics, historical Jesus and Synoptic Problem, Qumran and early Judaism, Christian origins beyond the New Testament, Islamic and late antique reception, global and critical interpretation, material culture, digital humanities, ethics, and a maintenance protocol.

The goal is to turn a curated bibliography into a research ecosystem. A research ecosystem must help a reader answer all of the following questions:

1. Which primary texts, critical editions, manuscripts, inscriptions, papyri, objects, images, maps, and datasets control this topic?
2. Which journals, indexes, review venues, and monograph series are the normal discovery points?
3. Which classic articles, state-of-the-question essays, and recent debate clusters shape the field?
4. Which non-English, global, minoritized, and confessional or non-confessional traditions are likely to be missed by an Anglophone book list?
5. Which methods, ethics constraints, and evidentiary risks govern responsible use of the material?
6. How should new items be evaluated, tagged, and maintained?

## Editorial principle

The existing repository can remain readable if the new files are treated as research layers rather than as simple appendices. Each book or topic should eventually include:

- **Primary evidence:** texts, editions, manuscripts, inscriptions, papyri, objects, material culture, datasets.
- **Reference infrastructure:** lexica, grammars, concordances, encyclopedias, databases, maps, catalogues.
- **Classic scholarship:** major monographs and landmark articles.
- **Current debates:** article clusters, special issues, recent review essays, and methodological disputes.
- **Global and critical approaches:** non-English, minoritized, and non-Western scholarship; feminist, womanist, queer, disability, ecological, postcolonial, decolonial, race, class, empire, and trauma work.
- **Reception and lived practice:** liturgy, art, music, preaching, popular culture, law, education, politics, devotional use.
- **Quality notes:** what is dated, superseded, contested, confessional, regionally constrained, or ethically sensitive.

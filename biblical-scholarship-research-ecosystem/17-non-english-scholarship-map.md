# 17. Non-English and International Scholarship Map

## Why this file is needed

A bibliography can look broad in English and still look provincial to international specialists. Biblical studies has major scholarly traditions in German, French, Hebrew, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, Scandinavian languages, Greek, Russian and Slavic languages, Arabic, Syriac, Chinese, Korean, Japanese, and many African languages. This file gives a practical map for integrating non-English scholarship into every subfield.

## Editorial rule

Do not isolate non-English scholarship in this file only. Use this file as a harvesting guide, then cross-link works into the relevant book, method, and subfield files.

## Minimum non-English audit

For each major repository file, ask:

1. Does it include German scholarship where German scholarship is central?
2. Does it include French scholarship where French scholarship is central?
3. Does it include Hebrew scholarship for Hebrew Bible, DSS, rabbinics, Jewish reception, archaeology, or epigraphy?
4. Does it include Italian scholarship for Qumran, patristics, and Catholic exegesis where relevant?
5. Does it include Spanish/Portuguese scholarship for liberation, Latin American, and public Bible topics?
6. Does it include African, Asian, and Indigenous scholarship where the topic involves global reception or colonial interpretation?
7. Does it include scholarship in the language of the primary-source tradition when possible: Arabic, Syriac, Armenian, Georgian, Ethiopic, Coptic, Greek, Latin, Hebrew?

## Quality-control warnings

1. **Do not include token non-English titles.** They must be relevant and integrated.
2. **Do not assume English translations replace original scholarship.** Translation often filters debates.
3. **Do not omit article-level non-English scholarship.** Many important debates occur in journals and Festschriften.
4. **Do not overrepresent German and French while claiming global coverage.** Global South and Asian scholarship need active retrieval.
5. **Use local names and transliteration carefully.** Verify metadata in original script where possible.
6. **Record language tags.** This makes future coverage audits possible.

# 08. Material Culture, Digital Humanities, and Ethics

## Why this file is needed

Biblical scholarship cannot rely only on literary sources. Archaeology, epigraphy, papyrology, numismatics, iconography, manuscripts, GIS, environmental data, and digital corpora change how texts are dated, contextualized, and interpreted. At the same time, antiquities markets, forgeries, colonial excavation histories, and unprovenanced manuscripts create ethical and evidentiary risks.

## Ethics, provenance, and forgeries

### Non-negotiable issues

- Unprovenanced manuscripts and artifacts.
- Looting and illegal antiquities trade.
- Publication of private-collection objects.
- Museum acquisition histories.
- Colonial excavation and removal.
- Indigenous and local stakeholder rights.
- Human remains and burial contexts.
- Forged inscriptions, seals, bullae, ossuaries, and scroll fragments.
- Dead Sea Scrolls post-2002 fragments and forgery debates.
- Overconfident use of objects without archaeological context.

### Required metadata for artifact entries

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Provenance | Where was it excavated or first documented? |
| Chain of custody | Who owned or handled it? |
| Legal status | Was export/acquisition legal under relevant laws? |
| Publication history | Has it been peer-reviewed or only announced? |
| Material analysis | Has scientific testing been done? By whom? |
| Archaeological context | Is the context secure, disturbed, or absent? |
| Authentication debate | Are there credible forgery concerns? |
| Ethical note | Does citation risk legitimizing looting? |

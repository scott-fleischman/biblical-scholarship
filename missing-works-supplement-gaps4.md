# Remaining missing works and categories — supplemental gap audit (`gaps4.md`)

This file is intended as a drop-in supplement to the `biblical-scholarship` repository. It uses the same broad Markdown pattern as the repository: topical headings, short rationale, and tagged bibliography bullets.

This is a residual audit, not a duplicate of the existing lists. The repository already has substantial coverage of book-by-book scholarship, archaeology, linguistics, non-English controls, reception history, adjacent ancient cultures, hagiography, research infrastructure, and article-dialogue mapping. The entries below focus on categories and works that appear absent, under-foregrounded, or useful as explicit “quality-control” shelves.

## Key

* **[E] Essential / first-order anchor**
* **[I] Important / specialized anchor**
* **[H] Historical or classic anchor**
* **[A] Article or article cluster**
* **[T] Tool, database, corpus, edition, standard, or technical infrastructure**
* **[R] Reference infrastructure**
* **[NE] Non-English or original-language control**
* **[QC] Quality-control, reproducibility, provenance, or ethics control**

## Do not duplicate unless absent after line-by-line merge

The following areas already appear strong in the repository and should mostly be treated as cross-reference targets rather than new gaps:

* General linguistics, discourse linguistics, pragmatics, information structure, Biblical Hebrew, Greek, Aramaic, Syriac, Coptic, Geʿez, Arabic, Jewish languages, and translation linguistics.
* Core Levantine archaeology, survey, excavation reports, radiocarbon dating, ceramics, residue analysis, archaeobotany, zooarchaeology, and site-specific reports.
* General anthropology, ritual theory, narrative criticism, folklore, oral tradition, performance, and social memory.
* Non-English reference infrastructure, including German, French, Italian, Hebrew, and other control texts.
* Reception-history survey shelves, Reformation/early-modern Bible, vernacular Bibles, Bible societies, slavery/race, global reception, Bible in literature/film, and ethnographic Bible use.

## Highest-priority residual additions

* **[E/QC] Bibliographic metadata and authority-control shelf** — the repository is bibliography-heavy, so it should have explicit DOI/ISBN/ORCID/ROR/VIAF/WorldCat/Crossref/DataCite/OpenAlex checks, citekey policy, duplicate control, and review status.
* **[E/QC] AI and LLM use in biblical studies** — add a quality-control-first category: hallucination risk, source verification, prompt/audit trails, machine translation, classroom use, and AI-assisted bibliography construction.
* **[E/T] Computational philology beyond ordinary corpus linguistics** — strengthen stylometry, authorship attribution, text reuse, topic modeling, clustering, and reproducible code/data workflows.
* **[E/T] Digital critical editions and encoding standards** — TEI, EpiDoc, IIIF, CollateX, Stemmaweb, EVT, edition-review criteria, and data export should sit beside textual criticism and manuscript studies.
* **[E/QC] Open science, replication, and data ethics** — especially for computational source criticism, stylometry, aDNA, radiocarbon modeling, GIS, and machine-learning claims.
* **[E/T] Bible-translation workflow and technology** — modern translation theory is already present, but Paratext, eBible data, low-resource NLP, consultant workflows, and translation-ethics infrastructure need explicit coverage.
* **[E] Scientific archaeology addenda: aDNA and destructive-sampling ethics** — radiocarbon and archaeometry are present; add population-genetics controls and heritage/destructive-sampling protocols.
* **[E] Sermon, lectionary, cantillation, and sound history** — strengthen reception beyond commentary and doctrine: preached Bible, sung Bible, calendrical Bible, and chanted Bible.
* **[E] Economy, labor, slavery, household production, and class** — consolidate Hebrew Bible, Second Temple Judaism, New Testament, rabbinics, early Christianity, and Greco-Roman economic history.
* **[E] Cognitive science of religion, affect, emotion, and embodied cognition** — useful for ritual, memory, metaphor, purity, prophecy, conversion, martyrdom, apocalyptic experience, and reception.

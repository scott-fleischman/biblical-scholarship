Below is the **cross-cutting “missing shelf”**: the works you would still need even after reading major commentaries on every biblical, apocryphal, and Second Temple text. These are the books, articles, editions, dictionaries, and methodological works that define large scholarly questions across corpora.

**Key**

**[E] Essential/current anchor** — still central to serious work.
**[I] Important** — major, often specialized or one influential school.
**[H] Historical** — field-shaping, often partly superseded but necessary for understanding the debate.
**[T] Textual/source tool** — editions, dictionaries, manuscript tools, databases, critical apparatuses.
**[A] Ancient witness** — ancient Jewish, Christian, Greco-Roman, or rabbinic material relevant to modern questions.
**[R] Reference infrastructure** — encyclopedias, handbooks, bibliographies, orientation tools.

# 1. Reference infrastructure: what controls the whole field

These are not “arguments” so much as the reference ecosystem behind almost every major scholarly claim.

* **[R/E] *T&T Clark Encyclopedia of Second Temple Judaism*, ed. Daniel Gurtner and Loren Stuckenbruck** — probably the best compact reference ecosystem for Second Temple Judaism; over 600 scholarly articles across texts, history, practices, concepts, and material culture. ([Bloomsbury Publishing][1])
* **[R/E] *The Cambridge History of Judaism*, vols. 2–4** — indispensable for Persian, Hellenistic, Roman, and late antique Judaism; volume 3 treats the early Roman period from Pompey to Vespasian and beyond. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment][2])
* **[R/E] *The New Cambridge History of the Bible*, vol. 1: *From the Beginnings to 600*** — broad history of biblical languages, text production, transmission, canon, interpretation, and early reception. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment][3])
* **[R/E] *Anchor Yale Bible Dictionary*, ed. David Noel Freedman** — older now, but still one of the largest one-stop reference works, with six volumes and over 6,000 entries. ([Yale University Press][4])
* **[R/E] *Dictionary of Deities and Demons in the Bible*, ed. Karel van der Toorn, Bob Becking, Pieter van der Horst** — essential for divine beings, gods, angels, demons, spirits, and mythic figures. ([Brill][5])
* **[R/E] *Textual History of the Bible*, Brill** — the major current encyclopedic project for textual history; the Hebrew Bible volume alone contains hundreds of articles across about 2,000 pages. ([Brill][6])
* **[R/E] Orion Center Bibliography of the Dead Sea Scrolls and Associated Literature** — live DSS bibliography; the cumulative database now exceeds 21,000 entries. ([Orion Center][7])
* **[R/E] *Oxford Handbook of the Dead Sea Scrolls*, ed. Timothy Lim and John Collins** — organized around contested issues: Qumran archaeology, sectarian identity, history, textual criticism, interpretation, and later Jewish/Christian relations. ([OUP Academic][8])
* **[R/E] *Oxford Handbook of the Apocrypha*, ed. Gerbern Oegema** — major reference for deuterocanonical/apocryphal literature in early Judaism and Christianity. ([OUP Academic][9])
* **[R/E] *A Companion to Biblical Interpretation in Early Judaism*, ed. Matthias Henze** — particularly useful because it is organized by interpretive practices: rewritten Bible, Qumran, apocalyptic literature, wisdom, Hellenistic Judaism, and ancient exegesis. ([Accordance][10])
* **[R/E] *Oxford Encyclopedia of the Bible and Archaeology*** — archaeological reference organized around sites, regions, lifeways, material culture, and interpretive frameworks. ([Oxford University Press][11])
* **[R/I] *New Interpreter’s Dictionary of the Bible*** — useful broad dictionary for biblical, deuterocanonical, ANE, and Greco-Roman topics. ([Accordance][12])
* **[T/E] HALOT; DCH; BDB; TDOT; BDAG; LSJ; Muraoka, *Greek-English Lexicon of the Septuagint*; Sokoloff’s Aramaic dictionaries; Jastrow** — philological infrastructure.
* **[T/E] *The Context of Scripture*, ed. William Hallo and K. Lawson Younger** — now more important than Pritchard’s older ANET for comparative ANE texts.
* **[H/T] James B. Pritchard, ed., *Ancient Near Eastern Texts Relating to the Old Testament*** — historically decisive; still useful, but not the final control text anymore.

# 2. Textual criticism, textual plurality, and manuscript cultures

* **[H/E] Frank Moore Cross and Shemaryahu Talmon, eds., *Qumran and the History of the Biblical Text*** — classic collection for the effect of DSS on textual history.
* **[E/T] Brill, *Textual History of the Bible*** — the most important current encyclopedic textual-history resource.
* **[E/T] Göttingen Septuagint; Rahlfs-Hanhart Septuagint; NETS** — basic LXX infrastructure.
* **[E/T] Bruce Metzger, *The Text of the New Testament*; *A Textual Commentary on the Greek New Testament*** — still a standard NT textual-critical orientation.

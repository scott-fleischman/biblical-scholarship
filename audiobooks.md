Here is the high-confidence audiobook pass across the works I listed earlier. I treated a title as “confirmed” only where I found an Audible or LibriVox listing for that work, a clearly identifiable version, or a collection containing the work. I have not treated podcasts, lectures, summaries, or loosely related retellings as confirmed unless marked as a caveat.

Availability can vary by country and edition. LibriVox recordings use public-domain source texts and volunteer narration, so they are useful for access but not always the best scholarly edition.

⸻

1. Biblical texts, Bible translations, and major textual reception artifacts

Confirmed Bible audio editions

* King James Version / Authorized Version — available on LibriVox and Audible. LibriVox has complete and book-level KJV recordings; Audible has several complete KJV editions.  ￼
* Douay-Rheims Bible — available on LibriVox and Audible. LibriVox has Douay-Rheims Old Testament, New Testament, and deuterocanonical material; Audible also has complete Douay-Rheims editions.  ￼
* JPS Tanakh / Jewish Publication Society Tanakh — available on Audible. I found Audible listings for the JPS Tanakh, the JPS Torah, and individual JPS biblical books.  ￼
* Robert Alter, The Hebrew Bible: A Translation with Commentary — available on Audible. This is especially useful because Alter appeared in the earlier lists as a major literary biblical scholar and translator.  ￼
* NRSVue / New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition — available on Audible, including full-Bible and Apocrypha-related editions.  ￼
* NIV / New International Version — available on Audible, including complete Bible editions and dramatized/read editions.  ￼
* ESV / English Standard Version — available on Audible, including full-Bible editions.  ￼
* NASB / New American Standard Bible, including NASB 2020 — available on Audible, including complete Bible and Testament editions.  ￼

10. Highest-yield audiobook shortlist

For actual listening, the best “return on time” from the confirmed list would be:

1. KJV or Douay-Rheims Bible — for premodern/reformation reception.
2. JPS Tanakh or Robert Alter’s Hebrew Bible — for Jewish/modern literary translation reception.
3. 1 Enoch and Jubilees — for Second Temple Jewish reception.
4. Josephus, Antiquities and Jewish War — for Jewish historiographical reception.
5. Philo — with the caveat that the Audible versions I found are virtual-voice.
6. Apostolic Fathers collection — early Christian reception.
7. Irenaeus, Against Heresies — canon/rule-of-faith reception.
8. Origen, Against Celsus — Christian apologetic and scriptural defense.
9. Eusebius, Church History — canon and early Christian memory.
10. Augustine, Confessions, City of God, On Christian Doctrine — Latin Christian reception.
11. Maimonides, Guide for the Perplexed — medieval Jewish philosophical reception.
12. Aquinas, Catena Aurea and Summa Theologica — medieval Christian synthesis.
13. Luther, Bondage of the Will and Calvin, Institutes — Reformation reception.
14. Spinoza, Theologico-Political Treatise — birth of modern critical reception.
15. Strauss, Life of Jesus and Schweitzer, Quest of the Historical Jesus — modern historical Jesus reception.
16. Gunkel, Legends of Genesis — classic form-critical reception.
17. Finkelstein/Silberman, The Bible Unearthed — archaeology and popular critical reception.
18. Kugel, How to Read the Bible and Alter, Art of Biblical Narrative — modern Jewish/literary reception.
19. Levine, The Misunderstood Jew — Jewish-Christian reception.
20. Noll, Civil War as a Theological Crisis, Gutiérrez, Cone, and Delores Williams — modern political, liberation, Black, and womanist reception.

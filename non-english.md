Below is a **non-English / not-normally-accessed-in-English research map** across the same territory we have been building: biblical books, Second Temple Judaism, textual history, rabbinics, patristics, medieval reception, Reformation, modern reception, and global/contextual interpretation.

I use these additional tags:

**[NE]** No full English translation known to me, or not normally accessible in English.
**[OR]** English translation may exist, but the original-language form remains the scholarly control text.
**[SER]** Series, journal, encyclopedia, or edition to mine repeatedly.
**[P]** Primary-source edition or critical text.
**[S]** Secondary scholarship.

Translation status changes, so treat **[NE]** as “not normally available in English / should be checked in a current library catalogue,” not as an absolute legal claim about every excerpt or chapter.

# 1. First-order non-English reference infrastructure

These are the major non-English tools that a PhD student should know exist, even if they mainly work in English.

## German reference works

* **[E/R/SER][NE] *Theologische Realenzyklopädie* / TRE, 36 vols.** — a massive German theological encyclopedia, especially important for history of interpretation, patristics, Reformation, church history, dogmatics, and biblical-theological topics. De Gruyter describes it as a 36-volume specialist encyclopedia, 1976–2004, with more than 2,500 articles over more than 28,000 pages. ([De Gruyter Brill][1])
* **[E/R/SER][OR] *Religion in Geschichte und Gegenwart*, 4th ed. / RGG4** — essential German reference for religion, theology, Bible, Judaism, Christianity, reception, and history of scholarship. An English version exists as *Religion Past and Present*, but serious German scholarship still often cites RGG directly. Mohr Siebeck describes RGG4 as containing 15,665 articles and subarticles by 3,972 experts from 74 countries. ([Mohr Siebeck][2])
* **[E/R/SER][NE] *Reallexikon für Antike und Christentum* / RAC** — indispensable for late antiquity, patristics, early Christian material culture, biblical reception in ancient Christianity, and Jewish-Christian-pagan interfaces. The University of Cologne’s project page calls it an interdisciplinary standard work for theology, ancient history, classical philology, and archaeology. ([CCeH][3])
* **[E/R/SER][NE] *Lexikon für Theologie und Kirche* / LThK** — Catholic German encyclopedia; especially useful for Catholic exegesis, doctrine, councils, liturgy, patristics, medieval theology, and modern Catholic biblical reception.
* **[E/R/SER][NE] *Theologisches Wörterbuch zum Neuen Testament* / ThWNT, ed. Kittel/Friedrich** — translated as TDNT, but the German original remains important for the history of lexical-theological interpretation; use critically because of theological and historical problems.
* **[I/R/SER][NE] *Wissenschaftliches Bibellexikon im Internet* / WiBiLex** — current German online reference for biblical studies; useful for up-to-date German-language bibliographic orientation.

## French reference works

* **[E/R/SER][NE] *Dictionnaire de la Bible* and *Supplément au Dictionnaire de la Bible*** — old but still important for French Catholic biblical scholarship, ancient versions, archaeology, Second Temple Judaism, and reception. KU Leuven’s biblical studies guide notes the original five-volume project and the long-running *Supplément*, begun in 1928. ([KU Leuven Bibliotheken][4])
* **[E/R/SER][NE] *Dictionnaire de spiritualité ascétique et mystique*** — indispensable for medieval and early modern Christian biblical reception in monastic, mystical, liturgical, and devotional contexts.
* **[I/R/SER][NE] *Dictionnaire critique de théologie*** — useful for modern French Catholic and philosophical-theological reception.

## Hebrew reference works

* **[E/R/SER][NE] *Encyclopaedia Biblica* / אנציקלופדיה מקראית** — major Hebrew biblical encyclopedia; crucial for Israeli scholarship and older Hebrew critical Bible studies.
* **[E/R/SER][NE] *Mikra le-Yisrael* / מקרא לישראל** — modern Hebrew scientific commentary series combining textual criticism, language, ancient Near Eastern context, archaeology, literary analysis, Jewish tradition, and modern scholarship. Hebrew University/Magnes describes it as a scientific Bible commentary written by recognized scholars and using both traditional Jewish interpretation and modern biblical research. ([magnespress.co.il][5])
* **[I/R/SER][NE] *Olam ha-Tanakh* / עולם התנ״ך** — Israeli encyclopedia-commentary series with maps, diagrams, illustrations, historical-geographical notes, and interpretive essays; often useful for archaeological/geographical reception in Israeli pedagogy. Gefen describes it as widely used in Israeli schools and rich in maps, diagrams, and illustrations. ([Gefen Publishing][6])
* **[I/R/SER][NE/partial] *Daʿat Miqra* / דעת מקרא** — Orthodox Hebrew commentary series that integrates peshat, medieval Jewish exegesis, geography, grammar, and some modern research. Some volumes are being translated, but the Hebrew series remains the main resource. ([Gefen Publishing][7])

# 2. Hebrew Bible / Old Testament commentary series to add

These are the non-English commentary series a dissertation student should mine before assuming the English-language field is complete.

## German series

* **[E/C/SER][NE] *Biblischer Kommentar: Altes Testament* / BKAT** — one of the major German technical commentary series. It is often much fuller in composition history, philology, textual history, and redaction criticism than comparable English volumes. Use especially for **Genesis, Exodus, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Psalms, Chronicles, Qoheleth, Twelve**, and ongoing volumes.
* **[E/C/SER][NE] *Herders Theologischer Kommentar zum Alten Testament* / HThKAT** — major Catholic German series, often strong on final-form exegesis, textual detail, canonical shape, theology, and reception. Important volumes include **Eckart Otto on Deuteronomy**, **Georg Fischer on Jeremiah**, **Frank-Lothar Hossfeld / Erich Zenger on Psalms**, and others.
* **[E/C/SER][NE] *Handbuch zum Alten Testament* / HAT** — historically important German series with philological-historical focus. Mohr Siebeck says it covers the Hebrew canon and selected apocrypha, continuing the *Kurzer Handkommentar* tradition, with a philological-historical focus aimed at scholarly explanation of the Bible in its historical world. ([Mohr Siebeck][8])
* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Das Alte Testament Deutsch* / ATD** — less exhaustive than BKAT/HThKAT but historically important in German Protestant scholarship.
* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Zürcher Bibelkommentare: Altes Testament* / ZBK.AT** — concise but often sharp Swiss-German critical commentaries; useful for literary and historical synthesis.
* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Neue Echter Bibel* / NEB** — German Catholic commentary series; Echter describes it as the first long-running Catholic commentary on the Old Testament in the German-speaking world, meant especially to open the renewed liturgical readings of the OT. ([echter.de][9])
* **[H/C/SER][NE] *Kurzgefasstes exegetisches Handbuch zum Alten Testament*** — nineteenth-century German critical commentary tradition; useful historically for the development of source and philological criticism.

## French series

* **[E/C/SER][NE] *Commentaire de l’Ancien Testament* / CAT, Labor et Fides** — French Protestant/critical series; important for Francophone scholarship on Genesis, Kings, prophets, Ruth, Esther, and the Twelve. Eyrolles’ catalogue lists volumes such as Thomas Römer on Abraham and 2 Kings/Elisha in this series. ([Eyrolles][10])
* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Sources bibliques* and *Lectio Divina* volumes, Cerf** — often monographic rather than commentary-format, but important in French Catholic biblical scholarship.
* **[I/SER][NE] *Études Bibliques. Nouvelle Série*** — French-language monograph series, now associated with Peeters, with major studies on biblical books, Second Temple texts, and NT reception. Peeters lists volumes in the series such as studies on Elijah traditions and biblical formation. ([peeters-leuven.be][11])

## Hebrew series

* **[E/C/SER][NE] *Mikra le-Yisrael*** — indispensable for Hebrew-language academic study of biblical books; especially important for a student who wants Israeli scholarship rather than English-language summaries.
* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Daʿat Miqra*** — important for contemporary Orthodox peshat scholarship; useful in reception history because it shows how modern traditional Jewish exegesis absorbs philology, geography, and selected historical data.
* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Olam ha-Tanakh*** — less technical than *Mikra le-Yisrael*, but useful for Hebrew-language reception, pedagogy, and visual-geographical contextualization.

# 3. Hebrew Bible: major non-English monographs by topic

## Pentateuch and Torah formation

* **[E/S][NE] Erhard Blum, *Die Komposition der Vätergeschichte*** — foundational for the composition of the patriarchal narratives; essential for Genesis source/redaction debates beyond the English-language documentary-hypothesis literature.
* **[E/S][NE] Erhard Blum, *Studien zur Komposition des Pentateuch*** — one of the major German alternatives to classical documentary models; indispensable for Pentateuch formation.
* **[E/S][NE] Christoph Levin, *Der Jahwist*** — major German reconstruction of the Yahwist and non-priestly Pentateuchal composition; important even for scholars who reject his model.
* **[E/S][NE] Reinhard Achenbach, *Die Vollendung der Tora*** — central for late Pentateuchal redaction, especially Numbers and priestly/legal completion.
* **[E/S][NE] Jan Christian Gertz, *Tradition und Redaktion in der Exoduserzählung*** — crucial for Exodus composition, especially non-priestly and priestly narrative layers.
* **[E/S][NE] Eckart Otto, *Das Deuteronomium im Pentateuch und Hexateuch*** — key German work on Deuteronomy’s compositional and canonical location.
* **[E/C][NE] Eckart Otto, *Deuteronomium* HThKAT** — one of the largest and most important modern Deuteronomy commentaries; far more detailed in legal, redactional, and ancient Near Eastern discussion than most English commentaries.
* **[I/S][NE] Lothar Perlitt, *Bundestheologie im Alten Testament*** — important for covenant theology and Deuteronomic/Deuteronomistic concepts.
* **[I/S][NE] Norbert Lohfink, collected German essays on Deuteronomy** — many individual essays remain untranslated or hard to access in English; important for structure, covenant, law, and rhetoric.
* **[I/S][NE] Jean-Louis Ska, French and Italian studies on Pentateuch formation** — some work is translated, but much of his essay-level influence remains in French/Italian; useful for European synthesis of Pentateuchal criticism.

## Historical books / Deuteronomistic History

* **[E/S][NE] Rudolf Smend, *Die Entstehung des Alten Testaments* and essays on the Deuteronomistic History** — central for the Göttingen model of multiple Deuteronomistic redactions.
* **[E/S][NE] Walter Dietrich, *Prophetie und Geschichte* and Samuel/Kings studies** — crucial German scholarship on Deuteronomistic redaction and prophetic traditions.
* **[E/C][NE] Walter Dietrich, *Samuel* BKAT volumes** — major current German commentary for Samuel; strong on textual criticism, literary growth, and historical questions.
* **[I/C][NE] Volkmar Fritz, *Das Buch Josua* HAT** — concise but important German critical commentary on Joshua.
* **[I/C][NE] Ernst Würthwein, *Die Bücher der Könige* ATD** — important German commentary on Kings and Deuteronomistic redaction.
* **[E/C][NE] Thomas Willi, *Chronik* BKAT** — important technical German commentary on Chronicles; useful for genealogy, temple, and rewritten history.
* **[I/S][NE] Ernst Axel Knauf, German/French studies on Israelite history, Edom, and Persian-period Yehud** — important for historical-geographical and archaeological context.

## Prophets

* **[H/S][NE] Bernhard Duhm, *Das Buch Jesaia*** — classical German work behind modern Deutero-Isaiah and servant-song scholarship; not replaceable by summaries.
* **[E/S][NE] Odil Hannes Steck, *Bereitete Heimkehr* and Isaiah studies** — central for Isaiah redaction and exilic/postexilic composition.
* **[E/S][NE] Uwe Becker, Isaiah and prophecy studies** — important German work on prophetic book formation.
* **[E/C][NE/OR] Hans Wildberger, *Jesaja*** — translated in parts, but the German original remains an important control text for First Isaiah.
* **[E/C][NE] Georg Fischer, *Jeremia* HThKAT** — major German Catholic commentary; especially important for close reading and theological shape.
* **[E/S][NE] Hermann-Josef Stipp, Jeremiah textual and redaction studies** — crucial for the MT/LXX edition problem and Jeremiah’s compositional growth.
* **[E/S][NE] Karl-Friedrich Pohlmann, Ezekiel studies** — important German work on Ezekiel composition and theology.
* **[E/S][NE] Rainer Kessler, social history and prophet studies** — significant for prophetic social setting and ancient Israelite society.
* **[E/S][NE] Jörg Jeremias, German work on Hosea, Amos, and prophecy** — some English access exists, but the German corpus remains important for the Twelve and prophetic theology.
* **[I/S][NE] Aaron Schart, *Die Entstehung des Zwölfprophetenbuchs*** — major German work on the formation of the Book of the Twelve.
* **[I/S][NE] Jakob Wöhrle, Book of the Twelve studies** — current German compositional work on prophetic collections.

## Psalms, wisdom, and poetry

* **[E/C][NE/OR] Frank-Lothar Hossfeld and Erich Zenger, *Psalmen* HThKAT** — translated/adapted in Hermeneia for some volumes, but the German series is still a major control point for Psalms scholarship.
* **[E/S][NE] Bernd Janowski, German studies on Psalms, sacrifice, anthropology, and biblical theology** — important for cult, lament, anthropology, and theology.
* **[E/S][NE] Othmar Keel, German iconographic and Song/Psalms studies** — some English exists, but many iconographic studies remain German/French and are essential for visual-symbolic exegesis.
* **[E/C][NE] Ludger Schwienhorst-Schönberger, *Kohelet* HThKAT** — major German Catholic commentary on Ecclesiastes/Qoheleth.
* **[E/S][NE] Markus Witte, wisdom and Job studies** — important for German research on Job, wisdom, and late biblical literature.
* **[I/S][NE] Silvia Schroer, German works on iconography, gender, and religion in ancient Israel** — essential for material/visual/gendered approaches; not all major work is available in English.
* **[I/S][NE] Meir Weiss, Hebrew literary studies on biblical poetry and narrative** — significant in Israeli literary-critical scholarship.
* **[I/S][NE] Yair Zakovitch, Hebrew monographs on biblical narrative, inner-biblical interpretation, and reception** — some English exists, but many Hebrew works add examples and arguments absent from English summaries.

# 4. Septuagint and ancient versions

## Septuagint

* **[E/T/SER][P] *Septuaginta: Vetus Testamentum Graecum* / Göttingen Septuagint** — the major critical edition of the Greek Old Testament. The Göttingen project page states that for more than a hundred years the major critical edition of the Greek OT has been prepared in Göttingen. ([Göttinger Septuaginta][12])
* **[E/T/SER][NE] *La Bible d’Alexandrie*** — French translation and commentary series on the Septuagint; indispensable because its introductions and notes often offer book-specific LXX interpretation not duplicated in English. IOSCS notes nineteen published volumes of *La Bible d’Alexandrie* from Éditions du Cerf. ([CCAT][13])
* **[E/T/SER][NE] *Septuaginta Deutsch* / LXX.D** — first complete German translation of the Septuagint, with separate explanatory/commentary volumes; important for German-language LXX scholarship and translation decisions. The German Bible Society describes it as the first complete German translation of the Septuagint. ([Die Bibel][14])
* **[E/S][NE] Marguerite Harl, Gilles Dorival, Olivier Munnich, Cécile Dogniez, French LXX studies** — central for the French school of Septuagint scholarship and Alexandrian Jewish reception.
* **[E/S][NE] Martin Rösel, German LXX studies** — important for translation technique, theology of the Greek Bible, and LXX as interpretation.
* **[I/S][NE] Siegfried Kreuzer, German LXX and textual-history studies** — important for LXX textual criticism and Greek biblical history.
* **[I/S][NE] Natalio Fernández Marcos, Spanish studies on the Septuagint and biblical versions** — some English exists, but Spanish essays and editions add important detail.

## Latin, Syriac, Coptic, Armenian, Georgian, Ethiopic

* **[E/T/SER][P] *Vetus Latina: Die Reste der altlateinischen Bibel*** — primary critical series for Old Latin biblical witnesses; essential for patristic citation, Latin reception, and pre-Vulgate textual history. The Vetus Latina database is based on the Beuron card file of Old Latin citations from the Fathers. ([Brepols][15])
* **[E/T/SER][P] Beuron Vetus Latina Institute resources** — necessary for Old Latin textual criticism and biblical reception in early Latin Christianity.
* **[E/T/SER][P] CSCO / *Corpus Scriptorum Christianorum Orientalium*** — includes Syriac, Arabic, Coptic, Armenian, Georgian, and Ethiopic primary texts with scholarly apparatus; many volumes have Latin, French, German, or other non-English translation/commentary.
* **[I/S][NE] Sebastian Brock’s Syriac studies in German/French Festschriften and article collections** — English dominates Brock’s corpus, but Syriac scholarship itself often requires German/French apparatus and editions.
* **[I/S][NE] Michel van Esbroeck, Georgian/Armenian Christian literature studies** — important for oriental Christian textual reception.
* **[I/S][NE] Gérard Garitte, Georgian and Armenian liturgical/biblical studies** — older but important.
* **[I/S][NE] Alessandro Bausi, Ethiopic textual and manuscript studies in Italian/German/French venues** — important for Ethiopic Bible and pseudepigrapha.

# 5. New Testament commentary series and monographs

## German series

* **[E/C/SER][NE] *Evangelisch-Katholischer Kommentar zum Neuen Testament* / EKK** — one of the most important modern German NT commentary series. Some volumes have English translations or adaptations, but many major volumes do not. Essential for Matthew, Mark, Luke, Romans, 1 Corinthians, Galatians, Thessalonians, and Revelation. Ulrich Luz’s Matthew began in EKK and became a major international reference; the series is explicitly German-language. ([The Gospel Coalition][16])
* **[E/C/SER][NE] *Kritisch-exegetischer Kommentar über das Neue Testament* / KEK / Meyer-Kommentar** — historically foundational and still active in new form; De Gruyter lists current volumes including 2 Thessalonians, Colossians, and James. ([De Gruyter Brill][17])
* **[E/C/SER][NE] *Herders Theologischer Kommentar zum Neuen Testament* / HThKNT** — important German Catholic series with major volumes by Schnackenburg, Gnilka, Mussner, Oberlinner, Schlier, and others. Herder identifies the series and its editors as Joachim Gnilka, Rudolf Pesch, Heinz Schürmann, and Rudolf Schnackenburg. ([Herder][18])
* **[E/C/SER][NE] *Handbuch zum Neuen Testament* / HNT** — older but still important historical-critical German NT series. Mohr Siebeck states that the HNT was founded by Hans Lietzmann in 1906 and is based on historical-critical exegesis. ([Mohr Siebeck][19])
* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Regensburger Neues Testament*** — Catholic German commentary tradition; more theological/pastoral but important for reception.
* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Ökumenischer Taschenbuchkommentar zum Neuen Testament*** — concise German commentaries; useful for scholarly synthesis.

## French series

* **[E/C/SER][NE] *Commentaire du Nouveau Testament* / CNT, Labor et Fides** — important Francophone Protestant/critical NT commentary series.
* **[E/SER][NE] *Études Bibliques. Nouvelle Série*** — major French monograph series with many NT studies, including Johannine, Pauline, rhetorical, and intertextual works.
* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Lectio Divina* / Cerf** — important for French Catholic biblical scholarship and theology.
* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Cahiers Évangile*** — not dissertation-level technical commentary, but important in Francophone Catholic biblical pedagogy and reception.

## Italian and Spanish series

* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Commentario Paideia: Nuovo Testamento*** — Italian scholarly commentary tradition; important for Italian academic reception.
* **[I/C/SER][NE] *Commentario storico ed esegetico all’Antico e al Nuovo Testamento*** — Italian historical-exegetical series, with volumes on individual biblical books and deuterocanonical texts.
* **[I/T/SER][NE] *Nuova Versione della Bibbia dai Testi Antichi*** — Italian translation-commentary series with ancient text, philological apparatus, and exegetical-theological commentary; San Paolo describes the series as offering a new version with ancient texts facing, textual-philological apparatus, and broad exegetical-theological commentary. ([Edizioni San Paolo][20])
* **[I/SER][NE] *Biblioteca de Estudios Bíblicos*, Sígueme / Verbo Divino** — Spanish monograph series with biblical introductions, thematic studies, and book studies; Verbo Divino describes it as including monographs, introductions, and manuals with current data from contemporary exegesis. ([verbodivino.es][21])

## Key NT non-English works

* **[E/C][NE] Rudolf Pesch, *Das Markusevangelium* HThKNT** — major German Mark commentary; strong on tradition history, passion narrative, and early Christian community.
* **[E/C][NE/OR] Joachim Gnilka, *Das Matthäusevangelium* and *Das Evangelium nach Markus*** — important German Catholic critical commentaries; not fully replaced by English works.
* **[E/C][NE] Ulrich Wilckens, *Der Brief an die Römer* EKK** — major German Romans commentary; important for Paul, law, justification, and salvation history.
* **[E/C][NE] Michael Wolter, *Der Brief an die Römer* EKK** — newer German Romans commentary; important for contemporary German Pauline scholarship.
* **[E/C][NE/partial] Wolfgang Schrage, *Der erste Brief an die Korinther* EKK** — massive German 1 Corinthians commentary; some English access exists, but the German remains central.
* **[E/C][NE] François Vouga, French Pauline and Johannine studies** — important in Francophone NT interpretation.
* **[E/C][NE] Jean Zumstein, *L’Évangile selon saint Jean*** — major French Johannine commentary; especially useful for narrative, theology, and reception.
* **[E/S][NE] Jean-Noël Aletti, French studies on Pauline rhetoric and ecclesiology** — essential for rhetorical analysis of Paul in the Francophone tradition; one example is his *Essai sur l’ecclésiologie des lettres de saint Paul* in *Études Bibliques*. ([CEEOL][22])
* **[I/S][NE] Marie-Émile Boismard and Arnaud Lamouille, French Johannine and Synoptic source studies** — often idiosyncratic but historically important, especially for complex source theories; Lamouille’s *Un Évangile pré-johannique* appears in *Études Bibliques*. ([Google Books][23])
* **[I/S][NE] Pierre Benoit, French studies on Luke-Acts, Paul, and biblical theology** — important in mid-twentieth-century Catholic NT scholarship.
* **[I/S][NE] Daniel Marguerat, French narrative and Acts studies** — many works remain more influential in French than in English.

# 6. Second Temple Judaism, Pseudepigrapha, and Qumran

## Major non-English series and journals

* **[E/T/SER][NE] *Jüdische Schriften aus hellenistisch-römischer Zeit* / JSHRZ and JSHRZ Neue Folge** — German translations, introductions, and notes for apocrypha, pseudepigrapha, and other ancient Jewish texts. The new series presents ancient Jewish writings important for understanding early Judaism and early Christianity, with academically precise translations, introductions, and comments. ([De Gruyter Brill][24])
* **[E/J/SER][multi] *Revue de Qumrân*** — indispensable DSS journal; many articles are in French, German, Spanish, Italian, and English. Orion describes it as a leading international journal dedicated to Dead Sea Scrolls studies, founded by Jean Carmignac in 1958. ([orion.huji.ac.il][25])
* **[E/SER][NE] *Henoch*** — Italian-led international journal for Second Temple Judaism, apocalypticism, pseudepigrapha, and early Judaism.
* **[I/SER][NE] *Transeuphratène*** — French journal/series important for Persian-period and Second Temple history.
* **[I/SER][NE] *Études Bibliques. Nouvelle Série*** — many important French monographs on Second Temple, Qumran, and NT intertextuality.
* **[I/SER][multi] *Analecta Biblica*** — multilingual series of the Pontifical Biblical Institute; WorldCat records it as including English, French, or Italian volumes. ([WorldCat][26])

## Major non-English scholarship

* **[E/S][NE] Johann Maier, German Qumran translations and studies** — important in German-language DSS reception and interpretation.
* **[E/S][NE/OR] Émile Puech, French Qumran editions and studies** — essential for many fragmentary texts, resurrection, apocalypticism, and epigraphy.
* **[E/S][NE/OR] Florentino García Martínez, Spanish and French articles on Qumran** — many later translated, but the original Spanish/French corpus is important. Brill notes that *Qumranica Minora I* includes English translations of essays originally written in Spanish and French. ([De Gruyter Brill][27])
* **[E/S][NE] André Dupont-Sommer, French DSS studies** — historically important for early French Qumran interpretation.
* **[I/S][NE] Jean Carmignac, Qumran and NT studies** — historically important; also connected to *Revue de Qumrân*.
* **[I/S][NE] Marc Philonenko, French studies on 4 Ezra, apocalypticism, and pseudepigrapha** — important for Francophone Second Temple scholarship.
* **[E/S][NE] Paolo Sacchi, Italian work on apocalypticism and Second Temple Judaism** — important for Enochic and apocalyptic trajectories.
* **[E/S][NE] Gabriele Boccaccini, Italian/English Enochic Judaism scholarship** — many English works exist, but the Italian scholarly context matters for Enochic/Essene debates.
* **[I/S][NE] Hermann Lichtenberger, German studies on Qumran and early Judaism** — many articles remain in German.
* **[I/S][NE] Hartmut Stegemann, German Qumran studies** — important for reconstruction of scrolls and sectarian history.

# 7. Rabbinic literature, Targum, Masorah, and medieval Jewish interpretation

## Rabbinics and midrash

* **[E/T/S][NE] Saul Lieberman, *Tosefta ki-Feshutah* / תוספתא כפשוטה** — indispensable Hebrew commentary on the Tosefta; no English substitute.
* **[E/T/S][NE] Hanoch Albeck, *Shishah Sidrei Mishnah* commentary and introductions** — central Hebrew Mishnah commentary and scholarly introduction.
* **[E/S][NE] J. N. Epstein, *Mavo le-Nusaḥ ha-Mishnah* and *Mevo’ot le-Sifrut ha-Tannaim*** — foundational Hebrew scholarship on Mishnah text and tannaitic literature.
* **[E/T][NE] Theodor-Albeck edition of *Bereshit Rabbah*** — critical Hebrew edition; essential for Genesis Rabbah.
* **[E/T][NE] Mandelbaum edition of *Pesiqta de-Rav Kahana*** — essential critical edition.
* **[E/T][NE] Horovitz-Rabin editions of Sifre / Mekhilta traditions** — core textual control for halakhic midrash.
* **[E/T][NE] Finkelstein editions of Sifre Deuteronomy and related tannaitic midrashim** — critical edition work that remains central.
* **[E/T][NE] *Dikdukei Soferim* by Raphael Nathan Nata Rabbinovicz** — textual variants in the Talmud; historically important, still useful.
* **[I/S][NE] Ephraim E. Urbach, Hebrew works on the Sages and rabbinic theology** — some English exists, but Hebrew originals and essays matter.
* **[I/S][NE] Shamma Friedman, Hebrew studies on Talmudic sugyot and redaction** — indispensable for modern Talmudic philology.
* **[I/S][NE] Yaakov Sussmann, Hebrew studies on Mishnah, Talmud, and manuscript traditions** — essential for rabbinic textual history.
* **[I/S][NE] Menahem Kahana, Hebrew studies on tannaitic midrashim** — key current work.

## Masorah and Hebrew textual culture

* **[E/T][NE] Menachem Cohen, *Mikra’ot Gedolot Ha-Keter*** — essential Hebrew critical rabbinic Bible project based on the Aleppo Codex tradition.
* **[E/S][NE] Israel Yeivin, Hebrew Masorah studies** — some English exists, but Hebrew technical work remains important.
* **[E/S][NE] Yosef Ofer, Hebrew studies on Masorah and medieval biblical manuscripts** — current and important for Masoretic reception.
* **[I/S][NE] Aron Dotan, Hebrew studies on Ben Asher and Tiberian Masorah** — necessary for vocalization and accentuation research.
* **[I/S][NE] Malachi Beit-Arié, Hebrew codicology studies** — many works exist in English and Hebrew, but Hebrew scholarship is important for manuscript culture.

## Medieval Jewish exegesis

* **[E/S][NE] Sarah Kamin, *Rashi: peshuto shel miqra u-midrasho shel miqra*** — foundational Hebrew study of Rashi’s method, peshat, and midrash.
* **[E/S][NE/partial] Avraham Grossman, Hebrew works on Rashi, Rashbam, and medieval Jewish commentators** — some translated, but Hebrew originals are fuller and often more widely used in Israeli scholarship.
* **[E/S][NE] Uriel Simon, Hebrew studies on Ibn Ezra and medieval peshat** — important for Spanish-Jewish philology and exegesis.

# 14. What this adds that English-only work tends to miss

The biggest additions are not just “more citations.” They are different scholarly worlds:

**German scholarship** adds the densest source/redaction criticism, Deuteronomistic-history debate, prophetic-book formation, Reformation editions, Lutheran hermeneutics, and theological encyclopedic infrastructure.

**French scholarship** adds Septuagint-as-Jewish-Bible work, Catholic historical criticism, patristic and medieval exegesis, Jewish-Christian polemic, and philosophical hermeneutics.

**Hebrew scholarship** adds Israeli academic Bible interpretation, Jewish reception from within Hebrew philology, rabbinic textual criticism, Masorah, medieval Jewish exegesis, and modern Jewish Bible reception.

**Italian scholarship** adds Pontifical Biblical Institute work, Catholic exegesis, Second Temple/apocalyptic studies, patristic studies, and Italian commentary traditions.

**Spanish and Portuguese scholarship** adds Latin American liberation, popular Bible reading, decolonial interpretation, and ecclesial reception traditions that are often flattened in English-language summaries.

**Primary-source critical editions** add the real textual base: WA, CR, SC, GCS, CSEL, CSCO, Vetus Latina, Göttingen LXX, BHQ, ECM, Mikra’ot Gedolot Ha-Keter, and rabbinic critical editions. Without these, an English-language bibliography often gives the impression that reception history is mostly ideas, when it is also **editions, apparatuses, paratexts, manuscripts, translations, and scholarly lineages**.

Below is a **first-pass, high-density research map** for historical-critical and adjacent academic questions, organized mainly **book by book**. It is not a literal exhaustive bibliography; a fully exhaustive version across the Hebrew Bible, New Testament, Septuagint, Pseudepigrapha, and all Dead Sea Scrolls would require a database. For DSS alone, the Orion Center’s cumulative bibliography now has more than **21,000 entries**, so “all DSS literature” has to be represented here by the major literary compositions and core editions/commentaries. ([Orion Center][1])

**Key**

**[E] Essential/current anchor** — still a major orientation point.
**[I] Important** — significant, often specialized or one major school.
**[H] Historical** — field-shaping, sometimes superseded, still needed to understand the discussion.
**[T] Textual history / editions** — manuscripts, versions, textual criticism, critical editions.
**[A] Ancient / premodern witness** — ancient Jewish, rabbinic, patristic, or medieval sources relevant to modern questions.

Core control points for this whole map are **BHQ/BHS** for the Hebrew Bible, **Göttingen Septuagint / Rahlfs-Hanhart / NETS** for Greek Jewish scriptures, **ECM / NA28–NA29 / UBS** for the Greek New Testament, **DJD / DSSSE / Qimron** for Qumran, and major technical series such as **ICC, Hermeneia, Anchor Yale Bible, OTL, WBC, NIGTC, CEJL, JPS, Forms of the Old Testament Literature, SBL Septuagint and Cognate Studies**. BHQ aims at comprehensive documentation/evaluation of Hebrew Bible textual variants and includes book-level textual introductions; Göttingen is the major critical Septuagint project; ECM is now the most comprehensive critical edition for NT textual research and uses CBGM; ICC, Hermeneia, and Anchor Yale are among the major technical commentary ecosystems for historical-critical work. ([Die Bibel][2])

# I. Cross-book works you need before the book-by-book map

## A. Ancient and premodern witnesses

These do not answer modern historical-critical questions in modern form, but they often preserve early questions about authorship, canon, textual form, translation, chronology, and interpretive cruxes.

**Hebrew Bible / Jewish witnesses**

* **[A] Josephus, *Antiquities* 1–11; *Against Apion* 1** — canon, rewritten biblical history, chronology, apologetic historiography.
* **[A] Philo, especially *Questions and Answers on Genesis/Exodus*, *Allegorical Interpretation*, *Life of Moses*** — Hellenistic Jewish Pentateuchal exegesis.
* **[A] Dead Sea Scrolls biblical manuscripts and pesharim** — pre-Masoretic textual plurality, sectarian interpretation.
* **[A] Mishnah Yadayim 3; b. Bava Batra 14b–15a** — canonical debates, traditional authorship attributions.
* **[A] Targumim, Samaritan Pentateuch, Peshitta, Vulgate** — indispensable for textual history.

**Christian patristic witnesses**

* **[A] Origen, *Hexapla*** — foundational for LXX/Greek recensions; preserved fragmentarily.
* **[A] Eusebius, *Ecclesiastical History*** — NT canon, authorship traditions, reception.
* **[A] Jerome, biblical prefaces; *Hebrew Questions on Genesis*** — Hebrew/Greek textual comparison, canon, authorship.
* **[A] Augustine, *De doctrina christiana*, *City of God*** — canon, interpretive principles, textual issues.
* **[A] Theodoret, Chrysostom, Theodore of Mopsuestia, Ephrem** — ancient exegetical reception, sometimes historical observations.

# VII. Where to prioritize first

For a usable reading sequence, start with these **highest-yield anchors**:

1. **Textual control:** Tov, *Textual Criticism of the Hebrew Bible*; Ulrich, *Dead Sea Scrolls and the Origins of the Bible*; Göttingen/Rahlfs-Hanhart/NETS; Metzger/Parker/Wasserman-Gurry for NT; ECM where available.
2. **Hebrew Bible composition:** Wellhausen, Gunkel, Noth, von Rad, Carr, Baden, Schmid, Kratz, Levinson.
3. **Second Temple context:** Nickelsburg, Collins, VanderKam, Stone, Charlesworth, Henze, Stuckenbruck/Gurtner encyclopedia.
4. **NT historical-critical foundation:** Streeter, Wrede, Bultmann, Conzelmann, Sanders, Dunn, Martyn, Brown, Fitzmyer, Attridge, Koester, Aune.
5. **Book-specific technical commentaries:** use **Hermeneia, ICC, Anchor Yale, OTL, WBC, NIGTC, CEJL, JPS** as first-line commentary series, but select volume-by-volume because quality varies.
